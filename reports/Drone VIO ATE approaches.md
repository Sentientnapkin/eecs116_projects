# Fuse Geometric VIO With Learned Fallbacks

**Bottom line: the fastest credible route to ~0.5 m is a guarded hybrid, not a better learned velocity net.** On the server, the provided VINS-Mono (**0.475 m**) and OpenVINS (**0.553 m**) beat our learned family (B-D0 **4.55 m**) by about 8x, and our best frozen model (N2/kf_grid-input seed 217) is predicted at only ~3.9 m on the new server-calibrated benchmark, a single-anchor guess of ~4.2 m on the server ([calibration.txt](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/eecs116_projects/research/results/eval/server-benchmark/calibration.txt)). Learned error is broad and scales with flight extent (Spearman 0.88-0.93 with identity-pose error), dominated by yaw drift. Geometric VIO has about 0.6 m medians but catastrophic initialization tails on our pool (OpenVINS mean 28.5 m, VINS-Mono 5.5 m), and both server VIO scores sit below the 5th percentile of what our pool predicts for a 12-episode draw, so the server's 12 flights are friendlier to VIO than our pool. Because the metric is the raw mean, every solution is judged mostly by how it handles the worst ~10% of flights. Rank 1 is therefore a three-way guard (VINS-Mono, OpenVINS, N2 as a bounded-error fallback and velocity prior) with VIO initialization and takeover fixes. Rank 2 is re-training the learned family on server-matched 60 m, ~1 m/s flights, which our ~1,400 local flights never covered. Rank 3 is scoring the already-built yaw/direction guests. Nothing beyond the guard has a measured path to 0.5 m on the benchmark; expected gains below are my estimates, not results. Free arithmetic on existing per-flight scores (the oracle-min of the three guests) should run first, because it bounds what any guard can achieve.

## The benchmark: learned error is broad, VIO error is spiky

The dataset is the **server-calibrated benchmark** from commit a9f29e0: an 882-flight simulated VIOBench pool (441 static-start `fast-*`, 441 mid-flight-start `fastmid-*`), of which **171 flights** (86 mid-start, offsets 3.4-39.5 s) are the calibrated selection. Each flight is 60 s / 1200 frames at 20 Hz. The raw pool is in `/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/gen/raw-fast` (249 GB), the selection is hard-linked in `gen/raw-fast/selected`, the packaged release is `/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/baselines/bench-release` (54 GB), and a private HF draft `SentientNapkin/viobench-server-calibrated` exists but its upload was not verified. Per-guest scores sit in `/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/baselines/bench-fast/{selected,full}/<guest>-<hash>/scores.json`; only b_d0 and dead_reckoning have `full` (882-pool) scores. Selection is exponentially tilted on truth-only extent and path so the weighted extent is 4.526 m (server 4.694) and path 60.2 m (server 59.6), with effective sample only ~90 ([dataset notes](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/eecs116_projects/research/results/eval/server-benchmark/selection.txt)). Speeds come from planner overrides (cruise 3-6 m/s, high acceleration) because the default planner cannot exceed ~48 m in 60 s. A 6-flight mid-start smoke set is untracked in `gen/raw-midsmoke`. Whether the server's flights start mid-route is unknown, and the server spec is inferred from path/extent only.

The tails differ in kind between guest families ([failure-selected.txt](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/eecs116_projects/research/results/eval/server-benchmark/failure-pool.txt), plus the per-flight scores; metrics in metres):

For N2 the tail is thin: the worst flights (~9-10 m) are large-extent mid-start HSSD flights where the identity-pose error is already 8-12 m, so this is extent-driven drift rather than failure. For VIO the mean is 9-49x the median and three flights carry 52-77% of total error. VIO failures track initialization (OpenVINS mean 0.98 m if initialized within 3 s versus 168 m otherwise; VINS-Mono 3.39 versus 9.84), ProcTHOR scenes (OpenVINS 50 m versus 1.3-3.1 m on HSSD), and textureless or dark views (Spearman with flat fraction +0.59 to +0.61), not speed or extent. The two VIO systems share failures only partly (Spearman 0.64), and learned-model errors are uncorrelated with VIO errors (about 0.0). That last fact is what makes a guard attractive.

**Reporting recommendation.** Keep the raw mean unaligned ATE as the one official number. Alongside it always print the median, 95%-trimmed mean, 5/95 winsorized mean, count above a fixed 10 m cap, the worst-10% error share, and a 12-episode bootstrap range, because the server score is a 12-flight mean with a ~+/-0.8 m 90% range for learned guests, so differences under ~0.4 m should not be over-read. The outlier rule max(10 m, 3x the guest's median) is the notes' own proposal, not a repo convention; it removes at most 1 of 171 learned flights but 10-17 VIO flights. Use the trimmed view only to understand typical behavior; selecting methods on it would reward guests that win the median and lose the official mean, which is exactly the VIO trap.

## The 8x tension: server flights look easy for VIO, local ones do not

The server numbers are not noise around our pool numbers. Calibrating on extent and path alone, B-D0 lands inside its predicted range (calibrated 4.196 versus server 4.55; 12-draw range 3.46-5.06), but VINS-Mono (pool mean 4.7-5.5, 12-draw 90% range 0.56-18.3, server 0.475) and OpenVINS (31.2, range 0.66-175, server 0.553) fall at or below the bottom of their ranges, and dead_reckoning is also outside (server 9.07 versus 7.0). My reading: the server's twelve flights triggered no initialization divergence, and probably have more texture, better lighting and different scene statistics than our ProcTHOR-heavy pool (the release README itself calls the server "probably friendlier to VIO"). VIO server scores match the local VIO *medians* (0.58-0.62) almost exactly, so medians, not means, are the local numbers to compare with the server. The competing hypothesis, that VIO on the server is simply lucky, has probability on the order of 10-20% under the pool's own failure rates (my arithmetic: roughly 0.85-0.88 per flight without a >3 m failure, raised to the 12th power, and that must happen for both systems with partly shared causes), so plan for both worlds rather than bet on one. In the friendly world, a guard-hybrid inherits ~0.5 m. In the hostile world, the guard's value is the heavy-tail cap rather than the median.

The learned family's gap has two measured components. First, **yaw**: feeding TRUE body velocity through our own gyro attitude still gives 0.967 m on the campaign-full set, and perfect yaw cuts that to 0.209; ideal tilt alone barely helps (0.909), and gyro-bias correction via kf_grid already matches oracle bias (1.38 versus 1.29 on public), so bias is saturated ([APPROACHES.md](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/eecs116_projects/research/APPROACHES.md), [project history notes](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/eecs116_projects/research_notes/Drone%20VIO%20ATE%20approaches/project_history.md)). Second, **extent shift**: the best model scores ~0.9-1.0 m on local 18 m public and campaign sets yet ~3.5 m on the 60 m pool (terciles of extent: 2.35 / 3.17 / 5.01 m), and all ~1,400 locally generated flights before the fast pool were <=33 m. Velocity RMSE is ~0.15 m/s; over 60 s at ~1 m/s a modest velocity bias or a yaw error of a few degrees accumulates to metres. No yaw-versus-scale decomposition has yet been computed on the server-calibrated pool; it is the cheapest diagnostic that is still missing.

## What has been tried, and what has not

Across ~22 documented experiments the pattern is consistent. Pure learned-velocity improvements (data scaling, 21x faster compiled training, longer schedules, architecture swaps) saturated at ~0.9-1.1 m locally, each step 5-25% and architecture changes inside seed noise (up to 0.29 m dev spread). Post-processing without new geometric information failed: a fixed EKF over learned velocity regressed (dev 1.095 to 1.129), a learned gyro-bias head regressed (1.144), iterative refinement and small visual attitude correction gave under 2%. What worked were additions that bring real geometry: kf_grid visual gyro bias (public 1.936 to 1.383) and translation-direction fusion (campaign 1.642 to 1.381, a 16% gain, with Wasm/Python parity unresolved), then the retrained N2 kf-input guest, the best local result at 0.888 public and 1.006 campaign. Geometric VIO was tried three ways. Our own upstream OpenVINS wrapper scored 2.84 on campaign30 because dynamic initialization failed on 117 of 120 crops. The provided OpenVINS example scored 1.108 public but 32.6 campaign-full, with 27 of 120 replays above 5 m from late-init takeover transients (up to 25 m/s) and accel-bias runaways. An offline learned/OpenVINS splice found a post-hoc gate (accept the OpenVINS increment if within 0.3 m/s of the learned velocity) giving 1.376 mean with 4 replays above 5 m, but the pre-declared gate failed and the gate was picked after seeing results. VINS-Mono was built and ABI-checked and only now scored, on the new pool alone.

Untried, in the repo's own list, and highly relevant to the tension: uncertainty-weighted (NLL) fusion of learned velocity with the provided smoother, seeding VIO initialization with learned velocity, a takeover consistency check, amortizing the Ceres solve, and native trials of DM-VIO, ROVIO, Basalt and ORB-SLAM3 (the last only a "partial run"). Also never run: the rotor-drag R-squared test, loop-closure revisit analysis, int8 quantization, seed ensembling, a signed-lateral training pilot, and a 192-flight independent stress set. Most important, **nothing has been trained on server-like flights** (60 m path, ~1 m/s mean, mid-start), and the four structural guests (Manhattan yaw, direction correction, combined, control) are built and ABI-checked but have no dataset score. No N2-family guest has a server score, so the server's ratio for the best model is extrapolated from B-D0 alone. The 3.98 m server submission ("compact camera-direction plus blended speed") has no located source, which means the best actual server score of ours cannot be reproduced.

The ordering follows the evidence. Rank 1 is the only item whose mechanism directly targets the 8x gap and whose components already exist as wasm binaries; its two inputs are guests with uncorrelated errors, one bounded near 3.5 m and one with ~0.6 m medians, so the combination's ceiling is the per-flight minimum. It also does not depend on knowing whether the server resembles our pool. Rank 2 raises that ceiling by shrinking the VIO tail itself, and the repo's own analysis shows the failures are initialization transients, which are fixable (and a learned velocity is available for exactly that purpose). Rank 3 improves the fallback and should be run in parallel because it is the only unexplored high-leverage change to the learned family and uses the GPUs well. Rank 4 attacks the measured dominant error of the learned family and is already built. Ranks 6-9 are tuning-level or literature-driven with weaker evidence for our setup; ranks 10-12 mostly fail on wasm feasibility or expected payoff.

## Staged plan on 8x H100

**Stage 0 (hours, CPU only, no new data).** Using the existing scores and trajectories on the 171 selected flights, compute the per-flight oracle-min of N2, VINS-Mono and OpenVINS, and the best simple gate with a declared rule (the existing post-hoc gate was chosen after results, so repeat it pre-declared with cross-validation by scene). Run `research/structural_pipelines/score.py` on the pool for the four structural guests. Compute the yaw/scale/bias decomposition of N2's pool error using truth attitude and velocity oracles, extending `decompose.py` and `oracle.py`. Kill/continue rule: if oracle-min exceeds ~1.5 m, rank 1 cannot reach the goal and effort shifts to rank 2.

**Stage 1 (days 1-3, in parallel).** CPU lane (180 workers): instrument the 17 OpenVINS and 10 VINS-Mono flights above 10 m, implement init/takeover fixes (rank 2), and launch the VIO parameter search (rank 5) on the 882 pool with clean-scene holdout. GPU lane (GPU1-7; GPU0 hosts another service, ~2 GB used): generate ~500 server-like flights with the OptiX simulator and the K2 override, signed-lateral and mid-start, in scene splits disjoint from the benchmark (the benchmark's clean ProcTHOR scenes and the 84 reserved campaign-test flights stay untouched), then train the N2 recipe with one seed per GPU (4.83 s/epoch warm; DDP scaling is only 60% efficient at 4 GPUs so independent runs win). Include window lengths near full flight and a tail-weighted loss.

**Stage 2 (days 3-6).** Build the composite guest (rank 1) with pre-declared gate thresholds; add the NLL uncertainty head (repo's untried item 1) for output-level fusion; run the cheap falsifiers (drag R-squared, loop revisit, XFeat track survival) and one native trial each of DM-VIO and Basalt on 20 late-init replays only if rank 1-2 fall short. Score everything with three seeds (217/218/219) on the 171 pool using `bench_pipeline.sh`, and on the clean ProcTHOR subset separately.

**Stage 3 (days 6-8).** Freeze, rebuild in wasm with all parity fixes (the historical kf_grid port passed an empty mask to recoverPose and some fusion variants fail Python/Wasm accepted-update parity), run the official evaluator, and report the full table of statistics above. The user decides on server submission, ideally submitting the hybrid and one learned-only N2 so the server provides a second learned anchor for the calibration. Gate every stage with predeclared thresholds, as the repo requires.

## What is uncertain, and why the numbers need care

The calibration is thin. Only one learned guest, B-D0, has a server score; extrapolating its ratio (4.55/4.196, ~1.08) to N2 gives ~4.2 m, but with one anchor and a 12-flight server set the true value could plausibly be 3.2-5 m. The VIO anchors and dead_reckoning fall outside the pool's ranges, so the pool is not a faithful proxy for VIO, and server scene types and mid-start use remain unknown. The effective sample of the selection is ~90 flights, with the scene groups heavily "seen" or "dev" (learned numbers there are optimistic; clean ProcTHOR N2 is 2.917). Per-guest `full` scores exist for only two guests. The learned-versus-VIO error correlation of ~0 is measured on the pool, not the server. Local parity is also unfinished: native-to-Wasm visual path differs by up to 0.42 m position on one flight, the Python/Wasm accepted-update parity of the corrected direction variants fails, and the 3.98 m guest's source is missing. Literature caveats matter too: published EuRoC and DROID/DPVO numbers are mostly **aligned** ATE (position+yaw or Sim(3)), while ours is unaligned, which hides exactly the scale and yaw errors that dominate us (a Feb 2026 study on scale drift reached the same conclusion). AirIO's table is internally inconsistent (text claims 52.9/54.4% gains versus ~43/45% from the table), and its numbers came via a fetch tool and need checking against the PDF. Several 2026 arXiv IDs came only from search snippets and are unverified (DB-VIO 2607.22123 looked anomalous, plus 2605.17327, 2609.21186, 2610.14951, 2607.17171, 2606.09355, 2609.21707, 2603.21785); do not cite them before opening the papers. The tail-handling claims for VIO (that init failures dominate means) are inference from init-time correlations, not filter-level diagnosis, since no trajectories were inspected.

## Conclusion

The project's own evidence reframes the 0.5 m goal. Extensive learned-velocity work moved the local campaign number from 1.84 to 1.0 m yet the server gap remains, because the server's distinguishing feature is long, fast flights where yaw and extent drive error, while the geometric baselines (0.475 and 0.553) already sit at the goal on exactly those flights. The decision is therefore less "which new model" than "which guard stops VIO's rare catastrophic flight from costing more than the learned fallback's bounded ~4 m", and the first measurement (oracle-min and a declared gate on existing scores) costs hours, not weeks.

The honest ceiling is conditional: if the server truly resembles the friendly world its VIO scores imply, a guarded hybrid can plausibly land near 0.5-0.8 m; if it resembles our pool, the mean will be set by how well initialization failures are caught and by the learned fallback's improvement through server-matched training, probably landing in the 1-3 m range. Submitting a hybrid together with one learned-only N2 guest would resolve both the calibration weakness and which world the server is.
