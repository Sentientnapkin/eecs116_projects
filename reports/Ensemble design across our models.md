# Lag one twin filter behind linejac

Our strongest affordable ensemble is not a mix of our builds. It is **two copies of the submitted GSLV-linejac (LJA) back end sharing one front end**. The primary runs on its normal governed budget. The secondary, perturbed only in the back end, runs in the primary's idle frame time and is allowed to fall 2-5 frames behind. The output each frame is the **plain mean of the two positions**, with no smoothing observer and no feedback into either filter. Replaying stored runs puts this at **about −4% ATE at about 1.00x peak fuel, worth roughly +0.09 points**, taking the server result from 0.633 m to about 0.607 m at 4.2 M ([fusion note, score table](../research_notes/Ensemble%20design%20across%20our%20models/fusion_mechanics_fuel.md)). Everything larger loses score. Independent copies of the build, mixing old-generation builds (B17X, RSOFT, HM40, HFG, low-fuel rows), adding OpenVINS, and per-flight selection all give back more in fuel than they gain in accuracy. Mechanism diversity across our catalogue turns out to be mostly *quality* gaps: the weaker build fails on different flights, but averaging it in drags the result toward it. The usable diversity is the filter's own chaos. A 1% pixel-sigma or detector-threshold nudge flips which flights blow up, and that is as complementary as any change of mechanism we have built. One untested question decides the whole design: whether that chaos survives when both copies share a front end. A single three-replica run with only `PIXEL_STD` nudged answers it. The planned note on learned components was not completed because research ran out of usage. Learned members are therefore covered here only through what the complementarity replay and the earlier selection-and-fusion report measured. On that evidence they behave like replicas, not like a new source of diversity.

## Our catalogue offers chaos, not complementary mechanisms

The complementarity study scored per-flight `estimate.tum` files for every build family that has runs on shared local pools. It found mechanism diversity only on st2dev (156 flights), st2conf (84) and st3 (234). Those pools hold 15 families with three chaos replicas each, the stlong learned-net variants and one OpenVINS run ([complementarity note](../research_notes/Ensemble%20design%20across%20our%20models/complementarity.md)). Low correlation appears only **between generations**. On st2conf, GSLV against the no-line builds (RSOFT, HM40, HFG, B17X, BM40, EDGE, G24M, C30M, L8) has a per-flight log-ATE correlation of 0.50-0.57. Within the line generation the correlations are 0.71-0.93, and GSLV and LJA reach 0.93. On the harder dev pools even that gap closes: every pair on st2dev correlates at 0.72-0.96 and every pair on st3 at 0.77-0.96, because **starved flights are hard for every build** ([rep_st2conf](../research/single_model/ensemble/rep_st2conf.txt); [rep_st2dev](../research/single_model/ensemble/rep_st2dev.txt); [rep_st3](../research/single_model/ensemble/rep_st3.txt)).

The low-correlation pairs are a trap. All 17 pairs that pass "correlation below 0.6 and hindsight gain of at least 12%" are made of old, weak builds, such as B17X+EDGE (hindsight −19.9%, causal mean −9.0%). Their fused result of 0.41 m or more is still **worse than GSLV alone at 0.333 m**. When the best family is paired with a decorrelated old build, causal fusion makes things worse: GSLV+B17X is +7.9% on st2conf and +11.5% on st2dev, and GSLV+RSOFT is +5.9% ([rep_st2conf](../research/single_model/ensemble/rep_st2conf.txt); [shortlist.json](../research/single_model/ensemble/shortlist.json)). OpenVINS is the extreme case. It has the lowest correlation of anything (0.26-0.32), but its st2conf mean is **143 m**, with 36 of 84 flights above 3 m. Any causal fusion with it explodes, and even its hindsight gain is only 1.7-2.7% ([rep_st2conf](../research/single_model/ensemble/rep_st2conf.txt)). B17X also carries a 16 M peak, so it would quadruple fuel on top of degrading accuracy.

The diversity that does help is replica chaos. GSLV's three replicas correlate at 0.83-0.91 per flight, with an error-vector correlation of 0.72-0.80. That makes them **less correlated with each other than GSLV is with LJA** (0.93-0.96 / 0.87-0.89). A three-replica causal mean gives −5.7 to −9.1% across all eight pools, matching or beating every mechanism-diverse triple that includes GSLV (−2.9 to −8.6%) ([complementarity note §3](../research_notes/Ensemble%20design%20across%20our%20models/complementarity.md)). LJA replicas behave the same way: −6.1 to −8.4% on the main pools, and −11.4% on st1conf, where one flight drives the result. The one family that is partly decorrelated *and* close in quality is GSLM (r 0.75-0.79 against GSLV). Its contribution flips sign by pool: a GSLV+GSLM pair is −6.2% on st2conf but **+4.1% on fconf**, where GSLM is the weaker model (0.305 m against 0.270 m) ([vsprim.log](../research/single_model/ensemble/vsprim.log)). The earlier report's best tail evidence for mixing families, 3 GSLV + 3 GSLM cutting the st2conf worst 10% from 0.85 to 0.73 m, holds only on st2conf. The new replay does not reproduce it on fconf, and this report treats it as pool-specific.

The tail behaves worse than the mean. In hindsight, fusion still rescues tail flights: GSLV+GSLM+B17X takes the st2conf worst-10% mean from 0.703 to 0.543 m. Causal fusion of the same sets is neutral or harmful there. GSLV+GSLM moves the tail from 0.720 to 0.790, and GSLV+LJB4+GSLM to 0.839 ([rep_st2conf](../research/single_model/ensemble/rep_st2conf.txt)). Same-family replica means improve the tail only in proportion to the bulk, for example 0.777 to 0.755 pooled for three GSLV replicas ([fusion note T1](../research_notes/Ensemble%20design%20across%20our%20models/fusion_mechanics_fuel.md)). No stored family, learned variants included, fails on *different* tail flights while staying as accurate as LJA elsewhere. That is the property an ensemble would need to rescue the server's three bad episodes.

## The plain mean beats the course baseline's consensus

The course OpenVINS solution fuses three fixed-lag smoothers with an equal-weight geometric median (GM) plus a 2 s anti-jump observer. It reported a −45% median ATE drop for 2.3x fuel ([OpenVINS solution README](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/baselines/viobench-upstream/viobench/solution/examples/native/openvins/README.md)). Ported to Python and replayed on our replicas, **that observer costs +12.5% ATE on three GSLV replicas** (95% CI +8.0 to +16.4) against the member average, and +13.3% on fconf. The plain mean gives −7.0% [−8.6, −5.2] on st2conf and −5.7% on fconf ([cons_sum.log](../research/single_model/ensemble/cons_sum.log)). The penalty grows steadily with the observer's time constant: −0.3% at 0.5 s, +4.4% at 1 s, +25.8% at 4 s and +47.4% at 8 s. The mechanism is specific to our filters. Their position jumps are EKF corrections *toward* the truth, and the reported twist does not contain those corrections: finite-differenced position disagrees with the rotated twist by 0.39 m/s RMS, with a 99th percentile of 1.43 m/s. Any observer that predicts with velocity and bleeds in position therefore throws corrections away. Integrating the twist alone gives +232% ([fusion note T1](../research_notes/Ensemble%20design%20across%20our%20models/fusion_mechanics_fuel.md)). The baseline's observer was a fix for hypothesis switching between smooth smoothers. On our EKF outputs it acts as a low-pass filter on the corrections. This overturns the earlier report's suggestion to use a baseline-style consensus with its observer.

Among observer-free rules, the differences are small and consistent. For two members the mean is the only sensible choice, and it introduces no switching. For three or more, the mean and GM are within about a point of each other. The median beats the mean when members differ in quality, for example GSLV+GSLM+B17X at −5.3% against −0.6%. The coordinate median (−4.5%), a trimmed mean (−3.7%) and the medoid (−4.3%, a causal *selection* rule) all trail ([rep_st2conf](../research/single_model/ensemble/rep_st2conf.txt); [cons_sum.log](../research/single_model/ensemble/cons_sum.log)). Weighting members by a causal running average of their distance to the GM adds at most a point (−6.5% on fconf). Fixed-weight pair blends tuned on dev transfer to conf, with chosen weights of 0.4-0.7 and gains within a point of w = 0.5. So **there is no exploitable trust signal in member disagreement** when members are this correlated. Raw-ATE scoring makes the output's discontinuities free, so the fused pose does not need smoothing. The primary's twist can be reported unchanged.

Two richer fusion levels were also replayed, and both lost. **Feedback** fails: re-anchoring each member onto the consensus every 1 s or 5 s scored +2.1% to −3.8% on three replicas, against −5.5 to −5.9% with no feedback, because it collapses the diversity the average depends on. **Measurement-level velocity fusion** has little to add. The GM of member velocities is only 2-4% closer to ground-truth velocity than a single run (0.148 against 0.150-0.154 m/s RMS on st2conf), because members share the same velocity bias ([fusion note, fusion level](../research_notes/Ensemble%20design%20across%20our%20models/fusion_mechanics_fuel.md)). Hard switching has a consistent record of failure in our code. Multi-hypothesis startup splicing was worse than the baseline at every switch time (0.650 at 1 s, 0.682 at 3 s, against 0.618), because "hypotheses are only distinguishable after the damage is done" ([multihyp notes](../research/single_model/multihyp/NOTES.md)). The three-source guard fusion over VINS-Mono, OpenVINS and a learned net reached 0.99 m held out, against a per-flight oracle of 0.759, and was limited by "sustained source selection, not switch smoothing" ([guard_fusion README](../research/guard_fusion/README.md)).

## A rare fuel spike leaves room for exactly one lagged twin

The score charges the **peak** frame, and the submitted build's peak is a rare event. On LJA arithmetic the local pool peak is 4.3-5.0 M, but the 99th percentile is 3.2 M and the median 2.1-2.2 M. Only 1.3-1.5 frames per flight exceed 3.5 M, and mean slack is 52-58% of peak. Spikes are only weakly synchronized across replicas, with a per-frame fuel correlation of 0.37-0.38 ([fuelprof.log](../research/single_model/ensemble/fuelprof.log)). On peak frames the back end (update 1.59 M plus solve_dx 0.78 M) is about 63% of the frame, and the shareable front end (KLT, pyramid, detection, plus priors and map) is bracketed at 0.6-0.9 M ([audit notes](../research/single_model/audit/NOTES.md); [fuelpush notes](../research/single_model/fuelpush/NOTES.md)). A queueing model on the real per-frame profiles keeps the primary on time. It queues the secondary's back-end work first-in-first-out with a D-frame deadline. With that model, **K = 2 fits under the existing peak (1.00x) once D reaches 2-5 frames**, and K = 3 needs 1.07-1.31x depending on the front-end cost and the pool ([fusion note T4](../research_notes/Ensemble%20design%20across%20our%20models/fusion_mechanics_fuel.md)). The lag is cheap. A secondary used D frames late and carried forward with the primary's increment, s(t−D) + p(t) − p(t−D), loses about 0.1 percentage point of gain per frame of lag ([lag.log](../research/single_model/ensemble/lag.log)).

Two cheaper-looking alternatives fail. Splitting the budget so each member gets about 4.2/K M per frame pushes both filters into the governor regime that already measured badly for a single filter: +0.039 m at 3.6 M, +0.057 at 2.7 M and +0.313 at 1.8 M ([tier1m notes](../research/single_model/tier1m/NOTES.md)). That is a larger loss than any fusion gain. Sharing the front end without time-slicing still costs 1.5-1.6x for K = 2, which turns the score negative. The secondary must therefore be **lagged, not squeezed**. It should run work-conserving and never preempt the primary, under a budget of the primary's GOV_B minus the primary's use minus about 0.3 M. The margin covers the governor's measured 0.2-0.3 M overshoot, which would otherwise land on top of the primary's spike.

The score law is points = 0.28·log2(peak_old/peak_new) − 2.24·ln(ATE_new/ATE_old) ([LOOP_LOG](../research/single_model/LOOP_LOG.md)). Applied to the measured gains, it ranks the designs as follows.

|---|---|---|---|

The expected server effect is modest and spread across all flights. The −4% comes mostly from the bulk of flights. The worst 10% improve by about the same percentage, so the server tail episodes 004c, cd6d and 2149 (1.53, 2.03 and 1.78 m, about 70% of our server error) would improve by roughly 4% each rather than be rescued ([LOOP_LOG, "SERVER RESULT GSLV-linejac"](../research/single_model/LOOP_LOG.md)). K = 3 matches K = 2 only if the front end is at the expensive end of its bracket and the lag stays at 5 frames or fewer. Its expected net against K = 2 is −0.07 to 0.00 points, so it is not worth the extra scheduler risk. The ceiling for this family of designs is the oracle headroom: per-flight hindsight of −14 to −19% and a 1-5 s segment oracle of −16 to −27%. Reaching it would need a causal quality signal that captures most of that headroom, and none of the measured rules captures more than about 40% of even the hindsight best-of-3.

## Learned members behave like replicas, and the learned-components note is missing

The planned research note on learned components was not completed; the research ran out of usage before it was written. This section therefore rests only on the learned-model evidence in the complementarity replay and the earlier selection-and-fusion report. It cannot address the final student-teacher students directly. **No stlong final student (teacher_final, wide0-2) had in-filter estimates on disk** when the replay ran. Only the baseline, pilot and pilot_mm variants (SLBASE, SLPILOT, SLPMM, each GSLV with a retrained or distilled velocity net, about 7 M peak, one replica each) could be scored ([complementarity note §1](../research_notes/Ensemble%20design%20across%20our%20models/complementarity.md)).

Those learned variants **behave like chaos replicas of GSLV**, not like independent estimators. Their per-flight correlation with GSLV is 0.86-0.89 on st2dev and st3, with an error-vector correlation of 0.72-0.76. Their fusion gains do not transfer: LJA+SLPMM is −6.3% on st2dev but GSLV+SLPMM is −0.4% on st3. The best causal triple on st3 (GSLV+SLBASE+SLPILOT, median) gives −4.9%, which is less than three plain GSLV replicas (−6.7%) ([rep_st2dev](../research/single_model/ensemble/rep_st2dev.txt); [rep_st3](../research/single_model/ensemble/rep_st3.txt)). LJA+SLPMM with a third member does produce the largest causal *tail* gain with the best family on st2dev, +11.1% with KV1 and +7.5% with BM40 under a median. These rest on 8-23 tail flights per pool, where tail gains swing by ±10 points between pools. The only earlier learned-source ensemble on record, the guard fusion with a learned N2 net, missed its oracle by 30% for lack of sustained selection ([guard_fusion README](../research/guard_fusion/README.md)).

The earlier report's strongest learned lead is not an ensemble member at all. It is a learned *weight* inside the single filter. Where the filter's velocity error exceeds 1 m/s, the velocity net's error is 0.44 against the filter's 1.41 m/s. Net-versus-filter disagreement is the one signal shown to carry information during a failure, while NIS and Pv stay overconfident ([learnvel notes](../research/single_model/learnvel/NOTES.md)). The net is already fused through periodic re-anchoring, which is why member-velocity fusion adds only 2-4%. A ~1 k-MAC map from disagreement and starvation counters to the re-anchor sigma would cost essentially no peak fuel. Published analogues exist: learned noise covariances in AI-IMU ([arXiv 1904.06064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.06064)) and measurement-level switching between VIO and learned velocity ([arXiv 2606.21669](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21669)). It is untested on our data, and the missing note was meant to settle where the student-teacher nets stand against it. Until a final student is run in-filter on st2dev, st2conf and st3 with three replicas, the safe working assumption is that a learned-net variant adds replica-like diversity at a higher peak (~7 M) than an LJA twin. It should not displace the twin as the secondary.

## Four tests decide whether to build it

The design has one decisive unknown and three engineering unknowns, and each maps to a cheap test with a kill criterion. All four follow the loop's conventions: three replicas, paired whole-pool means, scene-bootstrap CIs, no per-difficulty bins, and holdout used once at the end ([learnvel notes](../research/single_model/learnvel/NOTES.md)).

**Test A: does the chaos survive a shared front end?** Every replica gain above includes a front-end nudge (`DETECT_MIN_SCORE` 1010/990) as well as the back-end `PIXEL_STD` nudge ([geomstarve bp.sh](../research/single_model/geomstarve/bp.sh)). Run three LJA replicas with only `PIXEL_STD` nudged, or with a back-end seed such as a chi² or row-subsampling offset, on st2conf and fconf. Then rerun `pairs.py`. **Go if the pair gain stays at about −4% or better.** If it collapses toward zero, the chaos lives in the tracker, a shared front end kills the ensemble, and separate front ends cost at least 1.65x, which is score-negative. Stop there. This test costs one batch of runs and no new code.

**Test B: front-end cost.** Itemize GSLV line extraction and the rest of the front end on peak and non-peak frames. The 0.6-0.9 M bracket is the largest uncertainty in the K = 3 ratio (1.1x against 1.3x). It moves K = 2 only at small lags.

**Test C: scheduler prototype.** Build the pausable, work-conserving secondary at roughly 80 k-instruction row granularity. Mid-update pauses in the Cholesky and solve_dx steps need new code. Measure on the fuel pools that the true co-scheduled peak is **≤ 1.02x the primary's** and that the secondary meets D ≤ 5 on at least 99% of frames. Kill if the overshoot cannot be held under the 0.3 M margin, because at 1.05x the gain falls to +0.07 points and at 1.1x it is under +0.05.

**Test D: end-to-end replay, then one server shot.** Run the integrated K = 2 build on st2dev, st3, st2conf and fconf with three replicas. Accept if the pooled ATE change is ≤ −3% with a CI excluding zero and peak ≤ 1.02x. Then run holdout once and submit. In parallel, the learned-weight lead needs its own gate before it competes for build time: train on st2dev + st3, test on st2conf/fconf, and require replayed velocity error at least 10% below the fixed re-anchor sigma on failure stretches. That gate depends on a fresh GSLV diagnostic logging run, because per-frame quality signals were not logged with the shared-pool estimates.

## Conclusion

Under a peak-fuel score, an ensemble's value comes from scheduling more than from choosing members. The members we already have are close to interchangeable once quality is held fixed. Mechanism differences that look large in code (numeric against analytic Jacobians, a distilled velocity net) produce less trajectory diversity than a 1% pixel-sigma nudge. Mechanism differences that do decorrelate (old builds, OpenVINS) do so by being worse. The lever is the shape of the fuel profile: a median frame at half the peak lets a second back end run for free, provided it accepts a few frames of lag and never touches the primary's spike. That turns "ensemble" from a 2-3x fuel proposition into a 1.0x one.

The ceiling matters as much as the plan. The best measured design is worth about +0.09 points and spreads its −4% evenly, so it does not change the server's three tail episodes. Hindsight and segment oracles show −16 to −27% headroom, but no causal rule we tried captures more than about 40% of the hindsight gain. Closing that gap needs a member that is right on the tail flights while the primary is wrong. Nothing in the current catalogue provides that, and the unwritten learned-components note was the place that question was meant to be answered. Test A should run first: if back-end-only chaos does not survive, no ensemble of our builds pays, and remaining effort belongs inside the single filter.
