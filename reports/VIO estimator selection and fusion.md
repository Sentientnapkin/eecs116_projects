# Combine our own filters, not OpenVINS

Pairing GSLV with OpenVINS cannot pay under the course score, whatever selects between them. Adding a ~4 G estimator to our 4.2 M build multiplies peak fuel by about 950. To break even, ATE would have to fall about 71%, to 0.18 m, which is far below any geometric baseline on the server. Our own local data also shows OpenVINS fails *more* often than GSLV on the flights that hurt us. Combinations of our own filter variants are viable only in narrow forms: replicas fail together, so a 3x self-ensemble buys only 5-7% causally against an 18% break-even. The designs worth building keep peak fuel near 1.0-1.5x and attack the three server tail episodes, which carry 70% of our error. In order: a tiny learned network that softly reweights measurements GSLV already makes (velocity-net re-anchor, line and feature noise); keyframe-to-keyframe re-tracking and a few persistent landmarks taken from OpenVINS; and only then the course baseline's geometric-median consensus, run over 2-3 *mechanism-diverse* back ends that share one frontend. The course "OpenVINS" baseline is itself a three-smoother consensus that cut its median ATE 45% for 2.3x compute. That trade would be score-positive for us too, if our variants were as diverse as its hypotheses. Four offline tests on logs we already have decide this before any estimator code is written.

## Peak fuel sets a 0.18 m bar for any OpenVINS pairing

The score is a log-log area over peak per-frame fuel and mean unaligned ATE. Our working conversion is points = 0.28·log2(peak_old/peak_new) − 2.24·ln(ATE_new/ATE_old) ([LOOP_LOG, TIER1M](../research/single_model/LOOP_LOG.md)). Setting the gain to zero gives a single break-even law: **multiplying peak fuel by k requires ATE to fall to k^−0.18 of its old value**. The table applies it to GSLV's server result, **0.633 m at 4.2 M peak** (p95 2.8 M) ([LOOP_LOG, "SERVER RESULT GSLV-linejac"](../research/single_model/LOOP_LOG.md)).

| Peak multiplier k | Fuel cost (points) | Break-even ATE change | GSLV server mean must reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1x | −0.04 | −1.7% | 0.622 m |
| 1.5x | −0.16 | −7.0% | 0.589 m |
| 2x | −0.28 | −11.8% | 0.559 m |
| 3x (3 replicas or variants) | −0.44 | −18.0% | 0.519 m |
| 6x (6-run ensemble) | −0.72 | −27.6% | 0.458 m |
| ~950x (add a 4 G OpenVINS) | −2.77 | −71% | 0.184 m |

The same arithmetic shows how far ahead of OpenVINS we already are. On the server, OpenVINS reaches **0.553 m at 3.96 G** and VINS-Mono 0.475 m at 3.40 G ([server anchors](../../.claude/projects/-data-backups-rganapa-Sebastian-eecs116-projects/memory/server-baseline-anchors.md)). GSLV's 13% worse ATE costs 0.30 points, but its roughly 940x lower fuel earns 2.77, a net lead of about **+2.5 points**. Matching OpenVINS's 0.553 m at our fuel would be worth +0.30 points, enough to pay for a 2.1x fuel increase and no more. The selector itself is free. A per-frame MLP on ten scalar signals costs about 1 k MAC, roughly 0.03% of 4.2 M. Any combination's cost lies entirely in what it selects among.

The table also shows where the gain has to come from. Our server error is concentrated: **episodes 004c, cd6d and 2149 score 1.53, 2.03 and 1.78 m**, 5.34 m of the 7.6 m total. The other nine average about 0.25 m ([LOOP_LOG, "SERVER RESULT GSLV-linejac"](../research/single_model/LOOP_LOG.md)). Halving those three tails would bring the mean to about 0.41 m, worth roughly +0.97 points, which would justify up to 11x peak fuel. Averaging that mostly improves already-good flights would not justify even 2x. A useful combination therefore has to change the outcome on the tail flights.

## Our measured ensembles fail on correlated tails

The tail flights have a known local signature. They are fast flights through untextured ProcTHOR rooms. Velocity is weakly observed there, 15-24% of accepted features are more than 2 px wrong yet pass chi2 because the covariance is large, and starved vision updates push the gyro bias past its physical range (bg_y reaching 0.038 against a physical 0.02). The result is 6-8° of tilt that leaks gravity into velocity ([FILTCONS notes](../research/single_model/filtcons/NOTES.md); [learnvel notes](../research/single_model/learnvel/NOTES.md)). Flights whose median live-track count falls below 15 average 1.73 m and carry 39% of the dev-set ATE sum ([geomstarve notes](../research/single_model/geomstarve/NOTES.md)). The failures are partly chaotic. The worst flight scored 6.2, 5.9 and 10.3 m across three replicas, and only 50-65% of failures persist across builds ([LOOP_LOG, "SCORECARD DONE"](../research/single_model/LOOP_LOG.md)). In principle, that chaos is what an ensemble could exploit.

**OpenVINS is no second opinion on this failure class.** On st2conf (84 hard, fast, starved flights), the local OpenVINS build scored a **mean of 143 m**: 36 flights exceeded 3 m and 23 exceeded 10 m, at 4.2 G peak. It beat GSLV on only 2 of the 84 flights, and even after discarding its blowups, the hindsight best-of-two was 0.271 m against GSLV's 0.276 m ([LOOP_LOG, "ENSEMBLE FEASIBILITY (a)"](../research/single_model/LOOP_LOG.md)). An earlier three-source causal guard over VINS-Mono, OpenVINS and a learned net reached 0.99 m held-out against a per-flight oracle of 0.759 m. Of its seven held-out flights above 5 m, four had both VIOs bad and three picked the wrong one. The guard's own verdict was that "the dominant remaining issue is sustained source selection, not switch smoothing" ([guard_fusion README](../research/guard_fusion/README.md)). One uncertainty remains: the server's 0.553 m for OpenVINS shows it does far better on the server mix than on our st2 sets. The local binary (`openvins-9b1b079`) may not match the server baseline exactly, and we cannot see OpenVINS's per-episode server errors. That uncertainty does not change the verdict, because the fuel bar above rules out the pairing even with a perfect selector.

**Self-ensembles are too correlated to pay.** Three GSLV chaos replicas, fused per frame by mean or median, improved st2conf from 0.333 to 0.310-0.318 m (−5 to −7%) and fconf from 0.270 to 0.255 m (−6%). Hindsight best-of-three reached −16%, and six runs mixing GSLV and GSLM reached −8% causally ([LOOP_LOG, "ENSEMBLE FEASIBILITY (b)"](../research/single_model/LOOP_LOG.md)). Every one of these falls short of the k=3 or k=6 break-even in the table. Recomputing from the stored per-flight files ([ensemble/](../research/single_model/ensemble/)) gives the reason: per-flight log-ATE correlates **0.80-0.90 between GSLV replicas**. Mean fusion moves the worst 10% of st2conf flights only from 0.85 to 0.82 m, while the bulk improves from 0.271 to 0.249 m, so the gain lands in the wrong place. Mixing families is the one lever that reaches the tail. With 3 GSLV + 3 GSLM runs, the worst 10% move from 0.85 to **0.73 m**. That is the strongest local evidence that diversity of *mechanism*, not of seed, is what a combination needs.

Startup is not a rescue either. Multi-hypothesis startup with K velocity priors had an oracle best-of-two of 0.485 against 0.618, yet the causal splice was worse than baseline at every switch time (0.650 at 1 s, 0.682 at 3 s). The perfect-switch bound decays to zero by 3 s, because "hypotheses are only distinguishable after the damage is done" ([multihyp notes](../research/single_model/multihyp/NOTES.md)). This is the core problem for any selector scored on raw, unaligned ATE: error accrued before a switch stays in the trajectory.

## A learned weight beats a learned switch

The published record matches ours. The only quantified learned-switching result for classical VO, Holtz et al.'s random-forest selector, beat the *single best* algorithm by just **3.5% translational RPE**, against 39.4% over the worst ([CMU RI](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/learning-a-context-dependent-switching-strategy-for-robust-visual-odometry)). In other words, selection mainly avoids catastrophes; it does not improve the average. Field systems that do switch, such as JPL's HeRO, run *heterogeneous* sensors "so that their failure scenarios do not overlap." They compose each source's incremental motion onto the fused state so the output never jumps, and they add a pose-jump check because covariance stays small while the estimate is wrong ([HeRO](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.09679); [NeBula](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.11470)). Two estimators on the same monocular camera share their failures, and so do two replicas of our filter. One more point is directly relevant to us: every "run the expensive estimator on demand" scheme cuts *average* compute, not *peak*. Under a peak-fuel score, an on-demand second estimator sets the peak whenever it fires, and it starts cold exactly when it is needed ([Pan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21083v2); [Yang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06187v2)).

The alternative with real evidence behind it is learned measurement weighting inside one filter. AI-IMU maps raw IMU signals to the noise covariances of a Kalman filter's pseudo-measurements and reports 1.10% translational error on KITTI ([arXiv 1904.06064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.06064)). IV-SLAM learns a context-dependent reprojection-noise model and reports more than 70% fewer tracking failures ([arXiv 2008.02760](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.02760v2)). Kulkarni et al. switch at the *measurement* level, feeding either VIO velocity or learned velocity into one filter under a three-ramp health score (feature count, SLAM features, max velocity std) with 0.40/0.70 hysteresis bands ([arXiv 2606.21669](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21669)). That is almost exactly GSLV's periodic velocity-net re-anchor, with a learned weight in place of a fixed one.

Our own failures point to the same conclusion. Every *hard* mode switch keyed on starvation made GSLV-family filters worse. Starved-only velocity random walk reached 0.840 against 0.631, starved-only Schmidt 0.749, tilt random walk 0.690/0.761 and Huber 0.708, and re-anchoring the net only while live tracks were low was neutral ([FILTCONS notes](../research/single_model/filtcons/NOTES.md); [learnvel notes](../research/single_model/learnvel/NOTES.md)). Every mechanism that paid was always-on and soft. Lines plus Manhattan constraints gave −31% on st2dev, periodic net re-anchoring −8.8 to −12.4%, and RSOFT reprojection soft-weighting −10 to −12% ([LOOP_LOG](../research/single_model/LOOP_LOG.md)). The signal map explains why. Starvation counters such as live tracks, accepted-feature rate (the best measured selector, 85-93% decision accuracy over 3-5 s windows), texture and line counts say "this flight is in starved conditions." NIS and the velocity covariance stay small during a runaway, with Pv 0.2-0.3, about 13 sigma overconfident. The one signal shown to carry information *during* a failure is net-versus-filter velocity disagreement. Where the filter's velocity error exceeds 1 m/s, the net's error is 0.44 against the filter's 1.41. A chi2 gate on that disagreement fails because it uses the overconfident Pv, so it "rejects the net exactly when the filter drifted" ([learnvel notes](../research/single_model/learnvel/NOTES.md); [multihyp notes](../research/single_model/multihyp/NOTES.md)). A small learned map from (disagreement, starvation counters, speed, |bg|) to the re-anchor sigma and the visual/line noise scale has not been tried. It costs about 1.0x fuel, so any reliable gain pays. Note for anyone reusing old logs: the `u20` accepted-feature counter is always zero because `tc_used` is never incremented. Use att − gate − tri instead ([FILTCONS notes](../research/single_model/filtcons/NOTES.md)).

## The course OpenVINS is a three-smoother vote

The 0.553 m server baseline is not stock OpenVINS. Two OpenVINS frontends each initialise Ceres fixed-lag smoothers *once* and then serve only to supply gyro bias for image warping. The three smoothers each solve a **2.4 s window**: 13 keyframes at 5 Hz, up to 100 landmarks admitted in spatially balanced cohorts, six Gauss-Newton iterations per update, continuous-time IMU preintegration and square-root marginalisation. Hypothesis one tracks ordinary frame-to-frame KLT. Hypothesis two matches each keyframe *directly* against the previous keyframe, replacing accumulated frame-to-frame coordinates. Hypothesis three shares the ordinary frontend but weights vision at a quarter strength (pixel sigma 1.5/Huber 1 versus 0.75/Huber 2) and keeps its own states and prior ([OpenVINS solution README](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/baselines/viobench-upstream/viobench/solution/examples/native/openvins/README.md)). Around these sit gyro-warped KLT with forward/backward checks, a rotation-only startup that lets the drone turn in place, and a deliberately **disabled disparity ZUPT**, because it "can mistake slow translation for rest and incorrectly absorb that motion into the IMU biases" (same source).

The consensus in `consensus_output.h` is simple and nearly free. Each frame it takes the **equal-weight geometric median** of the three positions and of the three world-frame velocities. For three points this has a closed form: if any triangle angle is at least 120°, the median is that vertex, so a single far outlier is ignored. Otherwise it is the Fermat point. Rotation is a Karcher mean that holds the last output if it fails to converge. A **causal anti-jump observer** then predicts position from the previous output plus trapezoidal median velocity, and pulls toward the median position with α = 1 − exp(−dt/2 s), about 2.5% per frame at 20 Hz. Reported velocity includes the correction rate, so pose and twist stay consistent, and a jump when the median changes hypothesis bleeds in over about 2 s. Nothing feeds back into the estimators. Measured on 8 local episodes against the earlier single smoother, median ATE fell **1.535 → 0.849 m (−45%)** and improved on 7 of 8 episodes, while median fuel rose **2.3x** ([OpenVINS solution README](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/baselines/viobench-upstream/viobench/solution/examples/native/openvins/README.md)). Under our break-even law, 2.3x needs only −14%, so that consensus earned roughly +1 point. The README is candid that these are "correlated hypotheses, not independent measurements." It publishes no ablation separating frontend diversity from averaging, and its single smoother started from a 1.5 m median, where our median is already about 0.24 m.

### Most OpenVINS filter tricks are already measured losses for us

We ported the stock-OpenVINS estimator tricks onto B6 earlier, and **nothing was adopted**. FEJ on position or the full state was destructive (fresh 1.86 m); FEJ on attitude only was harmless (+0.026); OC yaw-null cost +0.085; the OpenVINS baseline-ratio gate cut flights above 5 m from 8 to 5 but raised the mean by +0.036; CHI2 2.0 cost +0.178; and 8 clones cost +0.011 ([openvins_tricks notes](../research/single_model/openvins_tricks/NOTES.md); [LOOP_LOG](../research/single_model/LOOP_LOG.md)). The papers' gains came in regimes unlike ours. FEJ2 cut EuRoC mono ATE about 22% on a filter that had no consistency fix ([FEJ2](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10376064)). SLAM features roughly halved mono EuRoC ATE (0.148 → 0.079 m), but half of that came from one sequence, and OpenVINS's own drone-racing report notes that "few SLAM features are tracked during highly dynamic motion" ([OpenVINS ICRA 2020](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/c10.pdf); [OpenVINS FPV report](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/r10.pdf)). Online calibration gives no benefit with exact calibration, so it should not be ported. Those B6 tests ran on an older base, so a GSLV re-test is not strictly ruled out, but it deserves low priority.

The pieces that remain cheap and untested target our drift directly. **Direct keyframe-to-keyframe re-tracking** at 5 Hz, using frame-to-frame tracks as initial guesses, adds one extra KLT per keyframe and removes accumulated tracking drift. It differs from the time-sliced 5 Hz tracking (VIS4) that cost +0.04, because it adds to 20 Hz vision instead of replacing it. A **small persistent landmark set**, capped at 10-20 one-dimensional inverse-depth points or long-lived lines, is estimated to cost about 1 M extra operations at that cap ([Li & Mourikis](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss08/p31.pdf) cost model). It carries information across window boundaries in the starved stretches where corners live only about 0.4 s. A **ZUPT audit** costs nothing: our startup code includes a vision ZUPT that fires on de-rotated disparity below 0.4 px (`research/single_model/startup/v2.c`), the very test the baseline disables as hazardous for slow-moving drones. Whether it still fires after startup in GSLV should be checked. The full relinearised 2.4 s window is the baseline's other big advantage, but GSLV already runs an iterated update (`solve_dx`, about 23 k instructions per row), so the cheap analogue is largely in place ([One million fuel design notes](../research_notes/One%20million%20fuel%20VIO%20design/starved_vision_cheap_tracking.md)).

## Five designs ranked by expected score

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | OpenVINS transfers: keyframe-direct re-tracking at 5 Hz; 10-20 persistent landmarks/lines budgeted on non-clone frames; ZUPT audit | ~1.05-1.5x | −1 to −7% | Targets starved-scene drift; none measured on our base | Build in parallel; gate on st2 tails |
| 3 | Baseline-style consensus (geometric median + 2 s observer) over 2-3 mechanism-diverse back ends that share one frontend, e.g. GSLV, GSLM, and a quarter-weight-vision variant | ~1.5-2x | −7 to −12% | Mixed families cut the st2conf top 10% 0.85 → 0.73 m; replicas alone −5 to −7% | Only if T1/T2 clear break-even offline |
| 4 | Learned per-frame selection among full variants | 2-3x | −12 to −18% | Hindsight best-of-3 is only −16%; guard fusion failed on sustained selection | Reject |
| 5 | GSLV + OpenVINS (switched, gated or fused), or IMM over noise modes | ~950x / 1.3-2x | −71% / −4 to −12% | OpenVINS fails on our failure class; the modes an IMM would mix all measured neutral or worse | Reject |

The cost figures for designs 2 and 3 are our estimates. They assume the frontend is the shared expensive part. The measured MSCKF update averages about 1.13 M per frame (about 105 k instructions per row × 10.8 rows), and peak frames are clone frames with little slack ([One million fuel VIO design](One%20million%20fuel%20VIO%20design.md)). A second and third back end therefore add on the order of 1-2 M each unless they run on reduced row sets, which is why design 3 lands nearer 2x than 1.5x without time-slicing. Design 3 has one structural requirement. The median of three needs two of the three hypotheses to be right on the tail flights, and replicas fail together (r = 0.8-0.9). The consensus therefore helps only if at least one member fails on *different* flights. Mixing GSLM into the ensemble is the only measured hint that ours can.

## Four offline tests settle this before any build

Each test uses logged runs only: the stored `lv_glNP{,_r1,_r2}`, `lv_gl{,_r1,_r2}` and `lv_ovins` trajectories on st2conf and fconf, plus the TC_LOG, GS_DIAG and LV_DBG streams ([ensemble/ens.py](../research/single_model/ensemble/ens.py)). Evaluation follows the loop's conventions: 3 replicas, paired whole-pool means, scene-bootstrap CIs, no per-difficulty-bin comparisons, and no holdout use until one final shot ([learnvel notes](../research/single_model/learnvel/NOTES.md)).

| Test | What it computes | Go criterion | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 consensus replay | Port `consensus_output.h` to Python: 3-point geometric median (Weiszfeld for 6), velocities from logged twist or finite differences, 2 s observer. Run it on the 3-GSLV and 3-GSLV + 3-GSLM ensembles; report mean, top-10% and the count above 3 m | ≤ −18% at k=3; ≤ −7 to −12% for a shared-frontend variant at 1.5-2x | Design 3 |
| T2 diversity and oracle | Pairwise per-flight log-ATE correlation across all logged families (HM40, RSOFT, GSLM, GSLV, eB edgelets, net arms) on shared pools; per-frame and 1-5 s segment hindsight oracles, the segment one being the honest target since per-frame oracles exploit noise | A pair or triple with r < ~0.6 and segment-oracle best-of ≤ −12% | Design 3 member choice |
| T3 signal predictability (scene-disjoint) | Train on st2dev + st3, test on st2conf/fconf. Targets: (a) position-error growth above 0.3 m over the next 2 s; (b) "net velocity beats filter velocity", the oracle weight for re-anchor sigma | (a) AUC ≥ 0.8 with ≥ 1 s lead, or (b) replayed velocity error ≥ 10% below the fixed P10S3 sigma on failure stretches | Design 1 |
| T4 fuel slack | Per-frame distribution of (peak − frame fuel) on clone and non-clone frames | Enough non-clone slack to time-slice landmark updates or a second back end under 4.2 M | Designs 2 and 3 cost |

If T3 passes, build design 1 as a ~1 k-MAC MLP trained on T3 target (b), preflight it, and accept any gain of at least 2% whose CI excludes zero across st2dev + st3, st2conf and f3. Run design 2 in parallel with a target of −5% on st2 at ≤ 1.2x peak. Consider design 3 only if T1 and T2 both clear their bars. Two gaps limit the plan. T3 may need one fresh diagnostic logging run on dev sets, because most per-frame diagnostics were logged on older HM40, RSOFT and eB builds rather than GSLV. Our coefficients 0.28 and 2.24 are also a local approximation of the server's area formula, not a re-derivation.

## Conclusion

The question changes once fuel is priced in. "Which estimator is best right now" does not matter for this submission, because any second full estimator costs more score than a perfect choice could recover. What matters is whether our filter carries enough *independent* information during a starved, fast stretch to correct itself. The course baseline won its −45% by averaging hypotheses that differ in what they measure, with tracking accumulated per frame or matched directly between keyframes and vision weighted at full or quarter strength. Our replicas differ only in chaos, which is why they buy a third as much. The velocity net is the one component of ours shown to be right where the filter is wrong. That makes a learned weight on it the cheapest true "combination of estimators" available, at essentially zero fuel.

The second takeaway is about evidence. Our most reliable results are negative: hard switches, inflation modes, FEJ/OC and the OpenVINS pairing all failed when measured. The positive bets (learned weighting, keyframe-direct tracking, persistent landmarks, diverse-family consensus) rest on mechanism and literature, not on our data. The tests above are cheap precisely so that each bet is falsified offline before it costs a build cycle and a server submission.
