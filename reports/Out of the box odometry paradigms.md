# Fix Amplitude Collapse, Then Guard Everything

**Nothing out-of-the-box beats the guarded-fusion plan to the team's ~0.5 m goal, but the new gp+msckf result and the researchers' corrections change what the guard should guard against and what to test first.** The measured failure of the learned net is amplitude collapse, not yaw: on 40 of 171 pool flights N2 estimated 0.35 m/s against a true 0.91 m/s and a trajectory extent of 4.2 m against 11.4 m, scoring 3.65 m against 4.23 m for staying at the origin. The new course-server guest "gp + msckf" (EKF/MSCKF with a Gaussian-process bias estimator, video) scored **3.22 m mean unaligned ATE, 1.25 m median, 0.735 m mean Sim(3)**, with three episodes (11.3, 8.68, 7.97 m) carrying ~72% of the error and Sim(3) scale factors of 0.49, 0.63 and 0.73. A good geometric filter therefore fails on a few episodes in a scale-shrinking way that resembles N2's collapse; the cause in gp+msckf is unverified, and its wasm source is not in the repo. My ranking: (1) a learned guard and calibrated-covariance fusion over four estimators, (2) an amplitude/scale diagnosis and fix applied to every guest, (3) learned-velocity seeding of VIO initialization, (4) deep-SLAM/foundation-model teachers for hard-case mining only, (5) scene-map localization, conditional on an unknowable fact. The physical and structural shortcuts (drag, thrust, heading coupling, planar priors, loop closure, Manhattan yaw) are dead ends by measurement. First actions, all CPU and under a day: run `gp_msckf.wasm` on the 171-flight pool, compute per-flight speed slopes for all guests, and compute the oracle-min over five guests.

## The new server data point says the geometric filter is excellent until it shrinks

The gp+msckf per-episode errors are 2.94, 0.272, 2.74, 0.271, 0.723, 11.3, 7.97, 1.57, 8.68, 0.585, 0.918, 0.742 m (mean 3.22 m, median 1.25 m, max fuel 19.5M, 12/12 episodes completed, no filled frames). On the same 12 episodes the provided VINS-Mono scored 0.475 m and OpenVINS 0.553 m, against 4.55 m for B-D0, 3.98 m for the compact camera-direction plus blended-speed guest and 4.694 m for the identity pose. Removing the three worst episodes leaves nine averaging ~1.2 m. One correction to the supplied summary: only six episodes fall in 0.27-0.92 m (0.272, 0.271, 0.585, 0.723, 0.742, 0.918), seven are under 2 m and eight under 2.74 m, so "8 of 12 are 0.27-0.92 m" does not match the listed values. Even the good episodes are not VINS-grade: the typical gp+msckf error (median 1.25 m) sits well above the provided VIO scores.

The gap between unaligned (3.22 m) and Sim(3)-aligned (0.735 m) error is the informative number. The trajectory shape is mostly right, and a large share of the unaligned error is a global scale and rotation offset that alignment removes. The scale factors confirm it for the worst three: 0.49 means the estimate covers about half the true extent. The worst episode travelled only 39 m, against ~60 m typical for server flights ([server flight distribution](../research_notes/Drone%20VIO%20ATE%20approaches/) and the pool calibration), so its error is not simply a long flight accumulating drift. This resembles the amplitude collapse the estimation-formulations researcher measured in N2 ([estimation_formulations.md](../research_notes/Out%20of%20the%20box%20odometry%20paradigms/estimation_formulations.md)), but I treat the resemblance as a hypothesis. The mechanism in gp+msckf could be weak-parallax scale unobservability, a GP bias term absorbing true acceleration, a failed initialization, or something else entirely. No one has looked at its internals, and the wasm source is not in the repo, so the tests below work from outputs only.

Four implications follow. First, the guard idea gains a fourth, differently-failing guest. gp+msckf's errors are on episodes where the filter is stable but shrunk, whereas VINS/OpenVINS fail by divergence and N2 by regression to the mean; if its errors are uncorrelated with the others (the repo measured N2 versus VIO correlation near 0.0), the oracle-min over four guests drops further. Second, the fuel axis matters: the course score is the area gained on a log-log fuel-vs-ATE plot relative to baselines, and the reported 19.5M maximum fuel for gp+msckf appears far below the 1.7-4.0 billion per-frame figures the repo recorded for N2, VINS-Mono and OpenVINS (the units and aggregation of the two numbers are not confirmed to be comparable). If they are comparable, a cheap filter that cuts 3.22 m to ~1.5 m by fixing scale shrinkage could earn more plot area than a ~1e10-fuel triple guard that reaches the same ATE. Third, on a log ATE axis, removing three gross failures (4.5 m to 1 m is a factor 4.5) is worth far more than polishing 0.55 m to 0.45 m, which supports spending effort on tails. Fourth, the user's view that the local benchmark is good is supported: the server order of the provided guests (VINS 0.475 and OpenVINS 0.553 best, B-D0 and the camera-direction guest near 4, identity 4.69) is reproduced in spirit by the pool, whose B-D0 calibration landed inside its predicted range (4.196 calibrated versus 4.55 server). The VIO scores fell below the pool's 12-draw ranges, so the local pool is harsher on VIO than the server, and gp+msckf is a clean second anchor if it is run locally ([Drone VIO ATE approaches](Drone%20VIO%20ATE%20approaches.md)).

## Corrections that reconcile the earlier reports with the new notes

The earlier report said yaw dominates the learned error, resting on an oracle test in which TRUE body velocity went through the team's gyro attitude: 0.967 m, falling to 0.209 m with perfect yaw. The new pool diagnostic says the dominant error is amplitude. Kabsch rotation alignment (no scale) lowers N2 from 3.65 to only 3.40 m and B-D0 from 3.80 to 3.43 m, scale-only alignment gets N2 to about 3.2 m, the best-fit global scale is 1.64, and N2's estimated path is 21 m against ~55 m true ([estimation_formulations.md](../research_notes/Out%20of%20the%20box%20odometry%20paradigms/estimation_formulations.md)). The two findings are compatible: yaw matters when velocity is perfect, but velocity is not perfect, so yaw is the second-order term for the learned family on 60 m flights. Caveats: the amplitude result covers 40 of 171 flights (alphabetical, including mid-start flights), the frame convention was reverse-engineered and checked only by reproducing official scores (3.65 vs 3.51 for N2), the yaw oracle was done on a different set with true velocity, and the notes themselves flag a loose end: a 2.6x speed shortfall coexists with a best-fit scale of only 1.64, so shrinkage is not a pure global scale and a per-flight, speed-dependent regression-to-the-mean is the likelier shape.

Four other corrections tighten the earlier plan. Drag and thrust aiding is falsified: on 24 flights the regression of IMU specific force on body velocity has R-squared 0.0107 (x) and 0.0016 (y), truth-derived 1.8e-4, and truth body-frame horizontal specific force is ~0, so the simulator is a drag-free thrust-along-z quadrotor ([structural_priors_shortcuts.md](../research_notes/Out%20of%20the%20box%20odometry%20paradigms/structural_priors_shortcuts.md)). Heading-from-velocity coupling is circular for absolute yaw: yaw follows velocity heading (median gap 11 degrees, 60% within 20 degrees, lag ~0.5-1 s), but the world-frame velocity direction is computed with the same drifting yaw, and the non-holonomic yaw-rate estimate reaches only correlation 0.41 and R-squared 0.17 even with truth kinematics. Loop closure and self-built maps stay weak because only 3.5-4% of frames revisit a place seen more than 10 s earlier and the drone yaws at 32-40 degrees per second, so features are visible for only a few seconds and short-gap re-observation collapses to the existing sliding window. Scene-specific map localization (ACE and relatives) pays only if server scenes were seen in training, which is unknown; the benchmark has 168 distinct scene ids across 171 flights, about one flight per scene, so per-scene maps must come from separate training flights, not the pool ([map_based_localization.md](../research_notes/Out%20of%20the%20box%20odometry%20paradigms/map_based_localization.md)). One tension remains unresolved: the implementation plan states that about 101 pool flights are in scenes training saw, while the map note counts nearly one flight per scene id; "seen" must mean other training flights in the same scene, and its exact count was not re-verified here.

Two citation hygiene corrections. DBA-Fusion is [arXiv 2403.13714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13714) and DSAC* is [arXiv 2002.12324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12324v4); a first-guess 2012.09654 for DSAC* is an unrelated paper and must not be cited. Several IDs were never opened and should be treated as unverified: 2609.07116, 2608.29720, 2608.25427, 2107.05851, 1505.07427, 2606.24767, 2607.14009, 2602.18174, 2603.26740, 2504.13180, 2605.00233, 2104.10831; others were seen only as search snippets (2105.08257, 2207.09442, 2412.08909, 2411.03951, 2411.03928, 2412.20082, 2511.15645, 2505.11763, 2308.01557, 2303.01418, 2204.09171, 2309.13814, 2604.06830, 2511.20343). Finally, almost all literature accuracy figures are Sim(3)- or scale-aligned: MASt3R-SLAM's 0.041 m EuRoC average uses scaled alignment ([MASt3R-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12392)), DBA-Fusion reports percent of trajectory length on TUM-VI ([DBA-Fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13714)), and the Sim(3) 0.735 m of gp+msckf versus its unaligned 3.22 m shows how much alignment can hide. The literature is directional evidence only.

## Ranked paradigms: what is worth trying, with kill tests

The ranking below is mine, with gain estimates that are estimates, not results. Everything is judged on mean unaligned ATE, causal operation and C/C++ wasm, with GPUs used only for offline training.

**1. Learned guard and calibrated-covariance fusion over four estimators (N2, VINS-Mono, OpenVINS, gp+msckf).** This is the guarded-fusion plan made learned. The per-flight oracle minimum over the first three guests is already 0.759 m against 3.51/5.51/28.5 m individually, and a hand-tuned agreement guard reaches 1.74-1.87 m on saved trajectories with thresholds fit on the same flights, so the honest expectation is 1.8-2.5 m ([implementation plan](Drone%20VIO%20implementation%20plan.md)). The out-of-the-box upgrade is to train the gate and covariances end to end: a differentiable Kalman filter with learned measurement covariances recovers metric scale and avoids divergence on degraded EuRoC ([Wagstaff et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07207)), and DFIO jointly trains displacement and covariance heads, cutting mean translation error 13.2% against TLIO (pedestrian data, no ATE) ([arXiv 2611.13517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13517)). Training labels are unlimited in simulation (label equals each estimator's windowed error), and the loss can target the raw mean. Cheap test: fit a small classifier on trajectory-only features with leave-scene-out cross-validation; kill it if the cross-validated gate exceeds 2.0 m mean. Add gp+msckf trajectories to the pool first, since they cost no wasm rebuild.

**2. Amplitude and scale repair, applied to every guest.** This is the family the new evidence favours and the least explored. The learned net's collapse suggests a data and loss fix (training speeds p90 0.51 m/s against a pool of 1.58 m/s, windows of ~4.8 s against 60 s inference), a binned or mixture velocity head instead of an MSE head, and an IMU-anchored scale step that adapts one scalar per flight against accelerometer-integrated velocity. Adapting only nuisance parameters is the safe form of test-time training; RIO and UL-VIO show self-supervised adaptation can help but on pedestrian and KITTI data ([RIO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11676), [UL-VIO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13106)). The planner's cruise-speed prior (mean ~1.0 m/s after a 6 s ramp, per-flight mean spread 0.54-1.17 m/s) is only a weak absolute scale bound at roughly plus or minus 30%. Cheap tests: E1, regress estimated against true speed on all 171 flights for all guests including gp+msckf, with "slope near 0.4 for learned guests" meaning the loss-and-data fix is the cheapest win; E3, rescale N2 speeds to the planner mean and kill if the gain is under 5%; and for the TTT scalar, offline fit to 2 s accelerometer windows, killed if scale error does not fall below 20% of the oracle best-fit. A bigger long-window, extent-matched retraining is the heavy version, with the repo's own kill rule: stop if long-window plus server-speed data gives under 10% over N2 ([scaling_and_large_models.md](../research_notes/Out%20of%20the%20box%20odometry%20paradigms/scaling_and_large_models.md)).

**3. Learned-velocity seeding and takeover rejection in VIO initialization.** OpenVINS averages 0.98 m when initialized within 3 s and 168 m otherwise, so the heavy tail is largely initialization. Foundation-model initialization reports >90% success in under 1.2 s versus 3-4 s ([arXiv 2605.17327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17327)), but a controlled study found a learned prior changed mean APE in VINS by only -0.2% on KITTI and online extrinsic updates worsened APE 45-52% ([arXiv 2609.13777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13777)), so expect the gain on tails only. Use N2 velocity as the seed rather than a heavy depth model. Cheap test: on the 17 OpenVINS flights above 10 m, replace the first 3 s of velocity with N2's offline; drop it if fewer than half show a detectable late-init or takeover pattern.

**4. Deep-SLAM and feed-forward 3D models as teachers and hard-case miners, not guests.** None runs in wasm. DBA-Fusion gives metric, IMU-fused TUM-VI error of 0.42% of trajectory length versus 2.64% for visual-only DROID-SLAM ([DBA-Fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13714)), and it is the only candidate with metric output. For foundation models the IMU must supply scale and yaw: VI3 grounds VGGT with preintegration and cuts 8-frame RMSE from 1.130 to 0.358 m on short TartanAir clips, but fails under low parallax ([VI3 abstract](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.03824)), and a third-party table shows raw VGGT-SLAM at 3.7-3.96 m on EuRoC with two sequences failed (search-snippet only). Relative rotation is the only plausible deliverable, and only if it beats integrated gyro over 60 s. Cheap test: 20 pool flights including the worst VIO tails, DBA-Fusion and VGGT windows at 320x240; drop an arm if the oracle-min improves by under 5% or tails do not shrink. Integration is the cost, not compute.

**5. Scene-specific map localization (ACE, retrieval).** ACE maps train in under 5 minutes per scene with 4 MB maps and reach 97.1% of 7-Scenes frames within 5 cm and 5 degrees ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14059), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14059)), which is drift-free in yaw and scale. It is the highest ceiling for seen scenes and worthless for unseen ones, with a wrong-scene false accept potentially costing metres, and a stock ACE head is ~2.5 G MACs per frame against a 2e10 cap and 170 scenes would be ~700 MB of maps. Do not build it first; run the cheap precondition test instead: DINOv2-class retrieval from the first 5 s against a training bank, requiring top-1 above 90% on seen scenes and unseen-scene rejection AUROC above 0.9. Note that a start-pose-only fix is capped, since drift accumulates along the flight (my estimate; the injected-yaw-error test settles it in hours).

**6. Lower-ranked and speculative.** Multi-hypothesis yaw tracking has no VIO precedent found and targets only the secondary yaw term; inject yaw offsets into N2 trajectories and kill it if the real likelihood does not cut yaw error 30%. Mamba/SSM long-context models are the team's current class, and architecture swaps stayed inside seed noise. Equivariant filters (EqVIO, MSCEqF) help initialization transients rather than amplitude, so run them only if linearization is shown to cause the OpenVINS tails ([MSCEqF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11649)). Privileged-information distillation of causal students from a non-causal long-context teacher is plausible for 5-15% on N2 (estimate).

## Dead ends, with the measurements that kill them

The table lists what not to spend time on. All values are from the researchers' local measurements on 24 pool flights unless stated.

| Idea | Why it is dead | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Drag/thrust aiding (HDVIO-style) | Simulator has no drag; accelerometer carries no velocity information | R-squared 0.0107 (x), 0.0016 (y) on IMU; 1.8e-4 on truth |
| Heading-from-velocity yaw, NHC yaw rate | Circular for absolute yaw; weak even with truth | corr 0.41, R-squared 0.17 for yaw rate |
| Planar / constant-height prior, floor homography | Height varies; forward camera sees floor rarely | height std 0.52 m, mean abs vz 0.24 against 0.92 m/s horizontal |
| ZUPT | Almost no stops | 8.9% of frames under 0.2 m/s |
| Learned gravity/attitude net | Tilt is not the bottleneck | 0.967 to 0.909 m with ideal tilt |
| Relaxed loop closure, self-built map | Few revisits, fast yaw | 3.5-4% revisit frames; 32-40 degrees/s yaw |
| Manhattan/Atlanta yaw anchors | Weak line concentration; guests barely fire | R4 0.12-0.19 on lines, 0.28 trajectory; struct guests move N2 by under 0.003 m (3.505 vs 3.508) |
| Seed ensembling of N2 | Seeds nearly identical | correlation 0.966-0.976; best-of-3 3.32 vs 3.51 |
| Stereo MAC-VO, Gaussian-splat/NeRF at runtime, Reloc3r in wasm | Wrong sensor, or not wasm-feasible | stereo-only; Reloc3r ~0.43B parameters ([Reloc3r](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08376)) |

The physical-prior conclusions apply to this simulator only; the server flights use the same simulator per the calibration, but literature gains from real-drone aerodynamics (HDVIO reports up to 33% better motion estimation ([HDVIO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11429))) should not be assumed to transfer. The simulator's cleanliness (zero lever arm, axis-permutation extrinsics, no drag) also explains why geometric filters like gp+msckf do so well on most episodes: there is little unmodelled physics to disturb them.

## What to do first, in order

Day one is CPU-only and decides the plan. Run `gp_msckf.wasm` on the 171-flight pool to get a second calibration anchor (server 3.22 m is the target to reproduce, with the three-worst-episode pattern as the signature) and to obtain per-flight trajectories, with the caveat that the wasm source is not in the repo so internals cannot be inspected; then compute, for gp+msckf, N2, VINS-Mono, OpenVINS and B-D0, the per-flight speed slope against truth (E1) and the Sim(3) scale factor distribution, to test whether scale shrinkage on the pool matches the server's 0.49-0.73 and whether it is shared across guests. In parallel compute the oracle-min over all guests and the scene-grouped cross-validated gate. These three outputs separate three worlds: if shrinkage is common and speed-slope near 0.4 everywhere, the amplitude fix (item 2) leads; if guests' errors are uncorrelated and the oracle-min is below ~0.8 m, the learned guard (item 1) leads; if the OpenVINS and VINS tails show a detectable initialization pattern, item 3 leads. Items 4 and 5 start only after those results.

Plan around the metric as scored: the raw mean over 12 episodes with roughly plus or minus 0.8 m 90% range for learned guests, so differences under ~0.4 m should not be over-read, and the mean is dominated by three episodes, as gp+msckf just showed. Treat any pool-fit threshold as optimistic and keep a held-out scene split. Finally, keep the fuel axis in the evaluation harness: log both ATE and fuel for every candidate, since the plot area, not ATE alone, is what is scored.

## Conclusion

The most useful change in understanding is that the project has been treating yaw, velocity regression and VIO failure as separate problems, while the data show one repeated signature: estimators that are locally well-shaped but globally shrunk or rotated, with a handful of episodes carrying most of the error. gp+msckf joins N2 as an example, and its 0.735 m Sim(3) against 3.22 m unaligned makes the point precisely. That reframes "inventive" options: the highest-value ideas are not new sensors or priors, which the simulator's physics mostly closes off, but a learned referee and an explicit scale-restoring step, both of which wasm can carry cheaply and GPUs can train with unlimited labels.

What remains unknown is decisive and cheap to find out. Whether gp+msckf's shrinkage is a bias-estimator artifact, a weak-parallax effect or an initialization effect is unverified, as is whether the server's scenes were seen in training, and neither can be resolved by more literature. The first day of local runs resolves the first; the second should be handled by a hard catalog gate rather than assumed, so that any map-based gain is bounded by a fallback to the guard.
