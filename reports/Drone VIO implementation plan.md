# Staged Guarded Fusion Drives Drone ATE Down

**Build a causal three-way guard over VINS-Mono, OpenVINS and N2 first, fix the VIO failure modes second, and retrain the learned fallback third. This sequence is the only path with a measured route toward the team's ~0.5 m goal.** On the 171-flight server-calibrated selection, the per-flight oracle minimum of the three guests is **0.759 m** (median 0.464), against 5.51 m for VINS-Mono, 28.5 m for OpenVINS and 3.51 m for N2 seed 217 ([guard_fusion_design.md](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/eecs116_projects/research_notes/Drone%20VIO%20implementation%20plan/guard_fusion_design.md)). A causal VINS-vs-OpenVINS agreement guard with N2 as tie-breaker already reaches **1.74-1.87 m** on saved trajectories, but its thresholds were swept on the same 171 flights, so treat it as optimistic; the honest expectation after scene-grouped cross-validation is 1.8-2.5 m (my reading of the notes, an estimate). Everything else is second order: structural yaw guests moved N2 by under 0.003 m, loop closure has only 3.5-4% revisit frames, and seed ensembling is worth 1-3% because seeds correlate 0.97. The plan below has 13 work packages, about 330-480 engineering hours and 60-120 GPU-hours, over roughly 12-14 working days with parallel lanes. My central estimate for the final pool mean is **1.1-1.8 m**, reaching 0.5 m on the pool is unlikely (under 10%), and the 0.5-0.8 m outcome is plausible only if the server resembles the "friendly" world its VINS/OpenVINS scores (0.475/0.553 m) imply. Every gain figure below is an estimate unless it says "measured".

## The decision logic: why the guard leads and what the evidence rules out

Two measured facts set the ordering. First, the learned family is the wrong place to look for 0.5 m: N2 scores about 0.87 times the identity-pose error on the pool (about 0.33 times on local campaign flights), with errors scaling with path and extent, and every data, loss and architecture change in the project has delivered at most 1.2-1.5x ([learned_optimization.md](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/eecs116_projects/research_notes/Drone%20VIO%20implementation%20plan/learned_optimization.md)). The learned notes' own ceiling for the family is about 2.1-2.6 m if the speed-OOD hypothesis (H1) holds and 2.8-3.2 m if drift is path-proportional (H2); neither is near 0.5 m. Second, the geometric guests already have the right median (0.58-0.62 m, matching the server scores) and fail in a few catastrophic flights: three flights carry 52-77% of total VIO error, VIO and N2 errors are uncorrelated (about 0.0), and only about six flights have all three guests above 3 m. The best source per flight is VINS on 88, OpenVINS on 71 and N2 on just 12, so N2's job is a bounded fallback, not a competitor.

The guard's discriminating signal must therefore come from VINS-vs-OpenVINS agreement and VIO health, not from N2. N2 is a weak referee: at 3-6 m/s cruise a 10-15% velocity-scale error equals 0.3-0.9 m/s, the same size as VIO transients, and tight N2 gates (0.5 m/s) gave 3.4-3.6 m, no better than N2 alone. The agreement guard measured 1.865 (a=0.5, b=0.1), 1.851 (1.0, 0.2) and 1.741 (2.0, 0.3) with seven flights still above 5 m. Its median (0.89) is 1.44 times VINS's median (0.62), which is a measurable false-trip tax; on a friendly server draw that costs about 0.3 m, so the 0.5 m goal requires cutting false trips with health signals and transition smoothing. Pure init-time switching is not enough either: 2.8-3.3 m for VINS-else-N2, and OpenVINS-else-N2 explodes from 1.45 m at a 3 s cut to 21.6 m at 5 s.

Three things are closed by measurement. Structural guests: struct_combined 3.505, direction 3.505, manhattan 3.507 versus control 3.508, maximum per-flight change 0.038 m, because their gates almost never fire. Manhattan evidence in the scenes is weak (mean line concentration R4 0.19 ProcTHOR, 0.12 HSSD against 0.05 random; only 2 of 10 ProcTHOR and 0 of 10 HSSD flights strongly aligned) ([vio_fixes_and_yaw.md](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/eecs116_projects/research_notes/Drone%20VIO%20implementation%20plan/vio_fixes_and_yaw.md)). Loop closure: frames revisiting a place seen more than 10 s earlier are 4.0% (ProcTHOR) and 3.5% (HSSD), with zero flights above 30%, under the repo's own 30% kill line. Ensembles: oracle best-of-three seeds is 3.32 versus 3.51 mean. These are dropped from the plan, with one cheap exception noted in WP10.

## Staged timeline with gates and kill criteria

The stages below are ordered by dependency; the day numbers assume lanes run in parallel (CPU lane of 160 evaluation workers, GPU lane on GPUs 1-7, one analysis lane) and are estimates.

**Stage 0, hours, CPU only, no new runs (day 0-1).** Using the 13 guests' saved `estimate.tum` and `twist.txt` on the 171 flights: reproduce the three singles' `ate_full` to 1e-6 (the notes reproduced 5.506, 28.529 and 3.508), recompute the oracle bound and pairwise oracles (min(VINS,N2) 0.995, min(OV,N2) 1.146, min(VINS,OV) 2.769), list the seven remaining guard failures above 5 m and classify them (both VIOs agree but wrong, switch transient, N2 itself bad), and re-run the agreement guard with thresholds fixed in a predeclaration file and chosen by leave-scene-group-out cross-validation. Also run the free replays for WP3: apply the OV velocity guard (|v| above 6 m/s, |dv/dt| above 12 m/s squared, sustained disagreement with the other source) to saved OV velocities, and substitute N2-integrated origin for the late-init replays. Add the F modifier (0.5-1.0 s decaying offset at switches) as an on/off arm. Finish by consolidating `perflight.jsonl` and running the missing 711-flight pool passes only if a CPU lane is idle (about 1-1.5 hours estimated for N2, VINS and OpenVINS, roughly 25-100 GB depending on whether the 30 MB `trajectory.mp4` per run is deleted after scoring). Gate G0 passed already: oracle3 0.759 is below the 1.5 m threshold. **Gate G1 (predeclared): cross-validated held-out guard mean at or below 2.2 m, no worse than min of the three singles on at least 70% of flights, median at most 1.5 times VINS's.** Kill rule: if CV mean exceeds 2.5 m, shift effort to WP5 first; if above 3.0 m, drop the guard and rely on fixed VIOs.

**Stage 1, days 1-4.** CPU lane: WP4 telemetry builds and classification, then the WP5 fixes that do not need N2, with fault-injection tests (force `failureDetection` true at 20 s on a good flight; a +20 m/s velocity jump in a saved IMU stream; both must recover within 2 s and the post-reboot error must not exceed N2's alone). VINS-Mono's -1 is mostly a deliberate throw: upstream `failureDetection()` reboots, and the viobench wrapper throws on any reboot after metric output, which freezes the trajectory under the evaluator's hold-last policy. The re-anchoring fix is about 40 lines in `solution.cpp` lines 216-222 and is the cheapest large win. GPU lane: WP2 diagnostics (D1 true velocity through our attitude, D2 speed-binned bias, D3 revisit and oracle yaw-snap), then the T0 pilot (200 flights, 100 static and 100 mid-start, in scenes disjoint from the benchmark's clean and dev groups and the 84 reserved test flights). **Gate G2 (H1 test): adding T0 at 50/50 improves held-out clean+dev mean by at least 10%; otherwise drop "server-style data is the lever" and cap WP7-8 at T1.** Also kill the speed-bias claim if N2 under-predicts speed above 1 m/s by under 5%. Gate G3 (OV guard, WP5): mean over the 17 OV flights above 10 m falls below 5 m and no flight previously under 1 m worsens by more than 0.5 m.

**Stage 2, days 3-8.** WP7 (T1 then T2 data; training one seed per GPU on GPUs 1-7, two runs per GPU, because DDP efficiency is only 60% at 4 GPUs), WP8 screens at 12 epochs with seeds 217/218 and a 10% promotion bar, then 36-72 epochs with seeds 217/218/219 for the top arms, WP9, WP11 and the remaining WP5 fixes. Long-window arms must be compared at equal optimizer updates, not equal frames, since earlier w96/w128 regressions (1.446 and 1.459 versus 1.312) used equal scored frames and therefore fewer updates. Gate G4 (learned arm acceptance): three-seed mean improves at least 5% (about 0.18 m), every seed better, and clean+dev subset (70 flights) improves by a similar margin; no-regression guards (original dev, campaign dev, public) stay within 5% or are explicitly weighed against the server-regime benefit. Gate G5 (WP11): held-out improvement at least 20% and at least half the search-slice improvement.

**Stage 3, days 6-12.** WP6 composite guest (after the OpenCV spike), WP10 attitude hand-off, WP12 preflight. Gate G6: the composite's pool mean is within 5% of the offline emulation of the same rule, and Python-vs-wasm ATE within 1% on 10 flights for weight-only components. **Stage 4, days 12-14.** Freeze on the commit hash, run the full standard report on the 171, the 882 pool (or a fixed 300-flight stratified sample) and the clean subset, score the fresh 192-flight set once, and hand the package to the user. Submit the hybrid and one learned-only N2 together, because the server has a 12-flight mean with about +/-0.8 m 90% range for learned guests and no N2-family guest has a server score; the pair reveals which world the server is and gives a second learned calibration anchor ([Drone VIO ATE approaches.md](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/eecs116_projects/reports/Drone%20VIO%20ATE%20approaches.md)).

## Experiment spec, leaderboard and the overfitting guards

One immutable JSON spec per experiment (`campaign/specs/<arm>/<exp_id>.json`, `exp_id = <arm>-<name>-<spec_hash8>`) carries these sections: `arm` and `parent` (the control guest SHA for paired comparison); `hypothesis`; `build` (commit, recipe, language "c++", pinned dependencies); `train` (data manifest, config, seeds 217/218/219, GPUs, scene manifest); `guest` (wasm path and SHA256, filled by the build stage); `eval` (evaluator commit `9b1b07906aff5a3e6f00155e7aecf967d90aa960`, sets selected171, pool882, clean, workers capped at 160 so loaders keep about 30 cores); `gate` (declared-at timestamp that precedes the first score, metric `mean_unaligned_ate_m`, slice, comparator SHA, rule, kill); `preflight` list; `resources`; `overfit` (search slice, held-out slice, configurations evaluated). Workers write a `result.json` next to the artifact containing, per set, n, mean, median, 95%-trimmed, 5/95-winsorized, count above 10 m, worst-10% share, 12-flight bootstrap 5-95 range, draw-weighted mean, peak fuel and filled-flight count, plus per-group splits (clean/dev/seen, procthor/hssd, static/mid), paired difference versus parent with scene-bootstrap 90% interval, and gate pass. Claiming uses atomic `mkdir` locks with heartbeats (stale after 15 minutes) rather than concurrent appends to one NFS file; resume semantics already exist in `launch.py` (checkpoints), `bench_eval.py` (cached `report.json`) and `bench_pipeline.sh` (missing zips) ([harness_and_orchestration.md](/data/backups/rganapa/Sebastian/eecs116_projects/research_notes/Drone%20VIO%20implementation%20plan/harness_and_orchestration.md)).

The leaderboard is `campaign/leaderboard.csv` with a rendered `.md`, one row per (guest SHA, seed), ranked only on the raw mean unaligned ATE for the declared slice, and only rows with `status=final`, three seeds for learned arms and `preflight_pass=true`; everything else is labelled exploratory. Reference rows stay pinned: identity pose, dead_reckoning, B-D0, N2 seeds 217/218/219, VINS-Mono, OpenVINS, and the oracle-min row marked "bound, not a guest". The existing `research/loop/leaderboard.md` must not be reused as is, because it carries a `sim3_ate` column that conflicts with the metric rule. Missing or filled frames always count as origin so a failed episode is never dropped.

Overfitting is the main threat to the headline numbers because the effective sample is only about 90 flights, clean-scene flights are only 21 of 171 (N2 2.92 m versus dev 3.89 and seen 3.45; VINS 2.08/5.22/6.36; OpenVINS 33.9/51.1/16.5), and VINS and OpenVINS server scores (0.475, 0.553) sit below the pool's 12-draw 5th percentile (0.56 and 0.66), so the pool is not a faithful proxy for VIO. Eight guards are therefore built into `report.py` and the leaderboard rules. (1) Mandatory split tags on every flight (group, catalog, scene, start type, source pool); every report prints the overall table and per-group tables, and decisions on tuned arms use the clean plus dev-HSSD slice. (2) Scene-grouped cross-validation for every threshold or learned arbiter, with scenes never split across folds, because flights from one scene share textures and failure modes. (3) A search slice disjoint from the held-out slice: search on seen ProcTHOR and dev, hold out clean (384 pool flights), count configurations evaluated, and flag if held-out gain is under half the search gain. (4) Predeclared gate in the spec before the first score; arms that alter the rule afterward are tagged `post_hoc` and excluded from rank, which is exactly how the earlier 0.3 m/s learned-gate result (1.376 mean) should have been treated. (5) Training manifests must exclude clean scenes (ProcTHOR 150-161, 176-179) and the 84-flight reserved test split, enforced by the leaderboard. Note that 176-179 held a "quarantined confirmation" role earlier, so any tuning there makes them dev. (6) A fresh sealed final set: 192 new 60 s flights per the repo's stress-set design; the simulator exposes no scenes beyond those already assigned, so the interim substitute is regenerating flights with new seeds in the clean scenes before any tuning touches them. (7) Differences under about 0.4 m for learned guests are noise against a 12-flight server draw. (8) Two-anchor sanity: every pass re-runs B-D0 and VINS/OpenVINS so pool-to-server drift is visible, and arms that win only where VIO anchors disagree with the server are down-weighted.

## Preflight before any server upload

The notes propose one `preflight.sh <package-dir>` chaining twelve stop-at-first-failure checks and storing `preflight.json` with the package; the user always uploads, and no agent submits. In order: provenance freeze (clean checkout, compiler flags, weights and wasm SHA, byte-reproducible rebuild); C/C++-only and dependency check; the official five-step ABI check on the exact binary; determinism (two fresh-process runs bitwise equal); causality (600- and 1200-frame prefix equality on three episodes including one mid-start) plus a **future-poisoning test** that corrupts frames and IMU after frame k and requires identical output through k (not implemented anywhere yet); reset and re-init (first pose identity, `init()` twice reproduces); mid-flight start on at least 6 mid-start and 6 static flights with no NaN, no exception and no speed transient above a bound (OV produced up to 25 m/s transients historically); zero filled frames on all 171 (VINS-Mono currently fails this on 2 flights, so the check is meaningful); fuel peak below 1e10 per frame for 2x headroom under the 2e10 cap; native/Python-vs-wasm parity, recording known deltas for the N2 family; full standard report on 171, clean and pool with three seeds and the declared gate; and a package with SHAs, source, weights and report. Known unresolved parity (native-to-wasm up to 0.42 m on one flight, kf_grid accepted-update parity failing for corrected-direction variants, an empty mask passed to `recoverPose`) must be recorded as server uncertainty; the user's HANDOFF says parity work must not resume without a new request. The composite adds a new failure surface: each sub-estimator needs try/catch so one failure marks only that source dead, since any non-finite output, all-zero quaternion or non-zero status ends the episode and scores the remainder at origin.

## Conflicts and corrections among the sources

Several statements in the earlier report are stale or conflict with the newer notes and, per the precedence rule, the newer notes win.

The earlier report says the four structural guests "have no dataset score"; they are scored on the 171 selection (files written 2026-11-20 02:32) and change the mean by at most 0.005 m (control is byte-identical to n2_kfinputs_s217). The earlier report's "all three guests are already wasm, one composite guest" omitted the OpenCV 5.1 (N2 kf_grid) versus 4.12 (both VIOs) link conflict; the notes make it the dominant risk of WP6. The earlier report's Stage-0 kill threshold (oracle above 1.5 m) is met with margin (0.759), and its effort of 5-8 days for the guard applies to the offline version only.

## Conclusion

The sharpest implication of the new notes is that the project already holds nearly everything needed: trajectories for 13 guests on all 171 flights, a 0.759 m oracle that is in hand, and a guard that is within a factor of 2.3 of it before any new run. The work is therefore a sequencing problem.
