## Q1. What the course "OpenVINS" baseline actually is, and which of its pieces explain its server accuracy

### Takeaway
The course baseline that scores 0.553 m on the server is NOT stock OpenVINS: after startup its output comes from three Ceres fixed-lag visual-inertial smoothers (2.4 s, 13 keyframes at 5 Hz, up to 100 landmarks, 6 Gauss-Newton iterations each) fused by a robust 3-way consensus; the OpenVINS EKF (with its 50 SLAM features) only bootstraps the smoothers and supplies gyro bias for image warping. Per its own README the consensus over hypotheses alone cut median ATE 45% (1.535 -> 0.849 m on 8 local episodes) at 2.3x the compute; the iterated relinearisation in a 2.4 s window and rotation-aware startup are the other big pieces. None of these exist in stock OpenVINS, so the OpenVINS papers' ablations do not explain this baseline's score.

### Cited Findings
- Architecture: "Two OpenVINS frontends initialize three smoother hypotheses; their robust consensus reports the camera pose and twist"; OpenVINS 2.7 + Ceres 2.1.0 + Eigen 3.4.1, compiled to wasm32-wasip1, single-threaded (Ceres `NO_THREADS`) — `OVSOL/README.md`.
- The EKF is used once: "Each EKF supplies its smoother's initial state and covariance once. After that, each smoother uses its own raw image and IMU factors; repeatedly using the EKF posterior would count those measurements twice. The EKFs continue running to provide the gyro biases used for image warping." — `OVSOL/README.md`; code: `smoother.initialize(get_state())` then `smoother.feed_camera(...)` each frame, `solution.cpp` lines ~224-239.
- Smoother: "jointly refines poses, velocities, IMU biases and landmarks in a 2.4-second window: 13 keyframes at 5 Hz, up to 100 landmarks and six Ceres iterations per update", "calibrated reprojection factors with a robust loss and continuous-time IMU preintegration", "New tracks enter in spatially balanced cohorts" (20-cell occupancy-balanced admission), outputs between keyframes are IMU predictions; marginalisation is square-root (column-scaled QR on Jacobians) of complete landmark segments and the oldest IMU factor — `OVSOL/README.md`, `fixed_lag_vio.h` (`keyframe_interval = 0.2`, `maximum_nodes = 13`).
- Three hypotheses: (1) ordinary KLT tracking, (2) "direct keyframe tracking" (each smoother keyframe matched directly against the previous keyframe, replacing accumulated frame-to-frame coordinates), (3) ordinary tracking with weaker visual weight (pixel sigma 1.5 / Huber 1 vs 0.75 / Huber 2 → "one-quarter of the visual objective") — `OVSOL/README.md`.
- Consensus: equal-weight geometric median (closed-form Fermat point for 3 points) of positions and velocities in the first-camera frame, intrinsic rotation mean, then "a causal observer predicts position from velocity and applies position corrections with a two-second time constant"; no feedback into estimators — `OVSOL/README.md`, `consensus_output.h`.
- Measured effect of consensus vs the previous single smoother (which already had rotation-aware startup, gyro-compensated tracking, calibrated IMU noise), 8 local 120 s ProcTHOR episodes, unaligned ATE: median 1.535 -> 0.849 m (-45%), median 1 s RPE 0.123 -> 0.094 m (-23%), median final drift 1.597 -> 1.138 m; ATE improved on 7/8 (00001-01: 2.699 -> 0.790; 00000-00 regressed 0.818 -> 0.866) — `OVSOL/README.md` Results table.
- Measured cost: median per-episode median fuel 96 M -> 224 M per frame (2.3x); median p95 1.224 B -> 2.648 B — `OVSOL/README.md`. Server max fuel for openvins 3.96 B/frame, server mean ATE 0.553 m (vins_mono 0.475 m / 3.40 B) — `MEM`.
- EKF settings (used only for startup/bias): FEJ on, RK4, 11 clones, max_slam_features 50, max_slam_in_update 25, max_msckf_in_update 40, MSCKF features GLOBAL_3D, SLAM features ANCHORED_MSCKF_INVERSE_DEPTH, dt_slam_delay 1.0 s, sigma_pix 0.5, chi2 multiplier 1; tracker num_pts 150, FAST threshold 15, grid 5x4, min_px_dist 8, KLT, no histogram equalisation; no online calibration ("the intrinsics and extrinsics are exact") — `OVSOL/solution.cpp make_options`.
- Rotation-aware tracking: "KLT uses the integrated gyro rotation to warp each new image into the previous camera orientation before tracking. This handles patch deformation during wide-angle turns. Forward/backward consistency rejects mismatches, and only valid tracks enter epipolar RANSAC" — `OVSOL/README.md` (implemented in `patches/open_vins.patch`, `set_temporal_rotation` in `solution.cpp`).
- Rotation-only startup (`rotation_update.h`): "fits calibrated rotations to spatially distributed feature tracks over half-second windows. Accepted fits update relative orientation, relative position and zero linear velocity, allowing the drone to turn in place"; thresholds fit RMS <= 0.7 px, inlier fraction >= 0.65, >= 20 inliers, sigmas rot 0.003 rad / pos 0.02 m / vel 0.05 m/s; hands off permanently to full VIO once the rotation model fails on enough tracks — `OVSOL/rotation_update.h`, README.
- "The ordinary zero-velocity detector is disabled: its raw per-frame disparity test can mistake slow translation for rest and incorrectly absorb that motion into the IMU biases." — `OVSOL/README.md`.
- Static init: 0.8 s window (+0.1 s history), accel-bias prior kept at the simulator's uniform ±0.2 m/s² (sigma 0.2/sqrt(3)) because "gravity alone cannot distinguish horizontal bias from tilt"; dynamic init (Ceres MLE, 6 poses, min 5° parallax) used otherwise, with nonfinite/negative-depth rejection — `OVSOL/README.md`, `solution.cpp`.
- IMU noise correctness: white noise per-sample at 200 Hz divided by sqrt(200); bias random walks "must not be multiplied by sqrt(200)" — `OVSOL/README.md`.

### Inferences
- (Inference) Server accuracy is explained mainly by (a) relinearised batch estimation over a 2.4 s window with 100 landmarks (this is what an EKF lacks on fast flights: each MSCKF/SLAM update is linearised once, and fast motion + short tracks make the first linearisation poor), (b) the 3-hypothesis consensus (measured -45% median ATE locally), (c) startup handling (rotation-only phase + careful static init + no disparity-ZUPT), and (d) gyro-warped KLT with fwd/bwd checks for fast turns. SLAM features in the EKF are at most indirect (better initial state handed to the smoother).
- (Inference) The consensus result is a variance-reduction effect between correlated estimators that differ in tracking (accumulated vs keyframe-direct) and visual weight. Its value comes from hypotheses failing at different times; the 00001-01 case (2.70 -> 0.79) suggests a single estimator occasionally takes a bad branch and the median rejects it.
- (Estimate) Transfer cost to our filter: a 3-way consensus of three full copies of our MSCKF would be ~3x our cost (~12 M ops), over the 2x budget, but cheaper variants fit: run 2 extra *light* hypotheses that share the frontend (feature tracks are the expensive part in a wasm frontend) and differ only in update weighting/gating (e.g. sigma_pix x2, different chi2 multiplier, or with/without line/velocity aids). Filter cost per hypothesis with 60-80-dim state is ~0.3-1 M ops/frame (state-size arithmetic below), so 3 back-ends + 1 shared frontend could stay within ~6-8 M. The geometric median of 3 points and a 2 s-time-constant position observer cost <1 k ops.
- (Inference) "Direct keyframe tracking" (re-matching current keyframe against the previous keyframe using frame-to-frame tracks as initial guesses) reduces accumulated KLT drift; this is cheap (one extra KLT per keyframe at 5 Hz) and directly targets slow drift — a strong transfer candidate.
- (Inference) Our team's experience (startup offset locked in in the first 2-5 s, mid-flight starts much worse — `MEM`) matches the baseline's emphasis on rotation-only startup and refusing disparity-only ZUPT.

### Gaps
- The README gives no ablation of the smoother window length, iteration count, landmark count, keyframe-direct tracking alone, or rotation startup alone; only "previous single smoother" vs "consensus" is measured. Results are 8 local episodes, not the server set.
- No per-component fuel breakdown (frontend vs EKF vs Ceres) is published; I did not run the module.

## Q2. SLAM features (persistent anchored inverse-depth landmarks) vs pure MSCKF features: accuracy gain and cost

### Takeaway
In OpenVINS's own EuRoC test, adding up to 50 SLAM features roughly halved monocular average ATE (0.148 -> 0.079 m), but most of that gain came from one sequence where the pure-MSCKF version drifted badly (V1_03: 0.344 -> 0.063 m); excluding it the gain is ~17%, and for stereo it is nil (0.055 -> 0.054 m). It cost ~1.6x runtime (4.3x -> 2.7x realtime mono). Li & Mourikis found the opposite for accuracy (plain MSCKF most accurate; hybrid ≈ MSCKF once features are initialised from >= ~9 frames) and framed the hybrid as a compute optimisation. For a tiny filter, 8-15 SLAM features is affordable (estimate ~0.3-1.5 M ops/frame) and helps most during hover/slow motion and with long tracks — not obviously on fast flights with short tracks.

### Cited Findings
- OpenVINS setup for EuRoC: FEJ-MSCKF, window 11 clones, max 100 feature tracks/frame, max 50 SLAM landmarks; "SLAM landmarks refer to the visual features that can be tracked beyond the current window, kept in the state vector, and marginalized out when lost" — [OV20](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/c10.pdf).
- Table II of [OV20] (10-run mean ATE deg/m, V2_03 excluded): mono ov slam 0.699/0.058, 1.675/0.076, 2.542/0.063, 0.773/0.124, 1.538/0.074, average 1.445/0.079; mono ov vio 0.642/0.076, 1.766/0.096, 2.391/0.344, 1.164/0.121, 1.248/0.106, average 1.442/0.148; stereo ov slam avg 1.552/0.054; stereo ov vio avg 1.454/0.055 — [OV20](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/c10.pdf). Authors: "the addition of SLAM landmarks in our OpenVINS greatly reduces the drift in the monocular case, while it has a smaller impact on the stereo performance."
- Table III RPE (deg/m) mono slam vs mono vio: 8 m 0.661/0.074 vs 0.826/0.094; 48 m 1.289/0.122 vs 1.425/0.184 — [OV20](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/c10.pdf).
- Cost: "we found that Basalt outperformed all other algorithms, with our proposed method being limited by the visual-frontend implementation from OpenCV and SLAM feature update equally. On the first EurocMav dataset we could process at 2.7x/4.3x and 1.2x/1.9x realtime for our monocular SLAM/VIO, and stereo SLAM/VIO" (Xeon E3-1505M, single thread) — [OV20](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/c10.pdf).
- FPV drone racing: "We found that while few SLAM features are tracked during highly dynamic motion, the inward facing turns, take off and landing segments greatly benefited from the inclusion of these features"; KLT was chosen because "it allows for longer feature tracks which facilitates longer lived temporal SLAM features"; settings window 15, 120 features, 40 SLAM features — [OVFPV](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/r10.pdf).
- Representation: FPV report states SLAM features "are estimated in an anchored frame with an inverse depth representation" — [OVFPV](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/r10.pdf). Delayed initialisation collects more than two observations, uses Givens rotations to split the stacked system into a feature-error part (gives feature estimate, P_ff, P_xf) and a nullspace-projected MSCKF-like part used for a normal EKF update — [OVDOC-DELAY](https://docs.openvins.com/update-delay.html).
- Li & Mourikis hybrid: MSCKF cost per update dominated by O(Σℓ_i³) residual/Jacobian and gating terms plus O(r³/6 + r(15+6m)²) for gain/covariance, with r = 2(ℓ(1)+ℓ(2)+ℓ(3)) − 7 bounded by 6m−7; flop model f_k = α1 Σℓ_i³ + α2 (r+2s_k)³ + α3 (r+2s_k)(15+6m+3s_k)² for s_k SLAM features — [LM12](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss08/p31.pdf).
- Feature tracks are mostly short: 88% and 69% of features tracked in ≤5 images in two dataset segments; features still tracked when the window (length m) fills are moved to the state as SLAM features — [LM12](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss08/p31.pdf).
- Accuracy (simulation, 232 features/image, mean track 5.6 frames, 50 MC trials): "the plain MSCKF results in the highest accuracy" (no Gaussian assumption for feature errors; all measurements of a feature used jointly → better outlier detection and linearisation); "if the size of the sliding window increases above a moderate value (e.g., 9 in this case), the change in the accuracy is almost negligible"; they never let the threshold fall below 7 — [LM12](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss08/p31.pdf).
- Runtime: optimal hybrid 37.17% faster than MSCKF and 72.8% faster than EKF-SLAM (laptop sim); 45.8%/55.6% faster on a Galaxy S2 (33.78 ms/update); real car data (540 features/image, track 5.06 frames): 139 ms MSCKF, 774 ms EKF-SLAM, 77 ms hybrid; "accuracy of the MSCKF and the hybrid filter are similar, and substantially better than that of the EKF-SLAM" — [LM12](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss08/p31.pdf).
- The [OV20] reference list cites [LM12] as the origin of its SLAM-landmark notion — [OV20](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/c10.pdf).

### Inferences
- (Computed from [OV20] Table II) Excluding V1_03, mono ATE avg is 0.083 m (slam) vs 0.100 m (vio): ~17% gain; the remaining ~half of the headline gain is one sequence where pure MSCKF lost scale/drifted (0.344 m). So SLAM features act mostly as a robustness/anti-drift anchor on hard sequences, and help long-horizon RPE (48 m: −34%) more than short-horizon.
- (Inference) The two sources are consistent once regime is considered: LM12's car data has short tracks and many features (MSCKF already strong); EuRoC/hover/turn-in-place segments have long-lived features, where a persistent landmark carries information across window boundaries that a fixed-window MSCKF discards. That anti-drift mechanism is exactly "slow drift/offset", but on *fast low-texture* flights few features live long enough to become SLAM features ([OVFPV]).
- (Estimate, cost for our filter) State = 15 IMU (+ maybe a few aux) + 6·C clones. With C = 8 clones: n ≈ 63. Adding S anchored inverse-depth SLAM features with 1-D inverse depth (anchor bearing fixed) adds S states; full 3-D anchored inverse depth adds 3S. Covariance update cost ≈ 2·r·n² + n·r² (+ r³/3 for S⁻¹), propagation of cross terms ≈ 2·15·15·n.
  - S = 10 (3-D): n = 93; SLAM update r = 20 rows → ≈ 2·20·93² ≈ 0.35 M ops; plus their share in the MSCKF update (n grows → MSCKF update with r ≈ 60 grows from ≈ 2·60·63² ≈ 0.48 M to 2·60·93² ≈ 1.04 M). Total extra ≈ 0.9 M ops/frame.
  - S = 20 (3-D): n = 123 → MSCKF update ≈ 1.8 M, SLAM update r = 40 ≈ 1.2 M; extra ≈ 2.5 M ops/frame.
  - S = 20 with 1-D inverse depth (bearing fixed at anchor): n = 83 → extra ≈ 1 M ops/frame. These sit within 2x our budget; S ≈ 50 (OpenVINS default) with 3-D representation (n ≈ 213) would cost ≈ 5-7 M extra — too much.
- (Inference) Practical policy to port: promote only features that reached the window length and passed chi² (LM12/OV); cap S at ~10-20 with spatial balancing; anchor at the clone that is marginalised next and re-anchor (LM12 Algorithm 1 changes the anchor when the anchor clone is removed); marginalise on track loss (drop rows/cols, O(n²)); use a delay (OpenVINS uses 1.0 s in the course config, `dt_slam_delay`) so new SLAM points have parallax — important on fast flight to avoid admitting badly triangulated points.

### Gaps
- No published ablation of SLAM feature *count* (e.g. 10 vs 25 vs 50) or of representation (global XYZ vs anchored inverse depth vs anchored 1-D inverse depth) in OpenVINS papers I could access; OpenVINS docs pages on representations were not fetched within the call budget.
- No published SLAM-feature ablation on fast, low-texture drone flights (FPV report gives qualitative statements only, no ATE).

## Q3. Other components: FEJ, online calibration, ZUPT, init, feature count/grid, KLT vs descriptors, chi², cloning/keyframing — which matter most

### Takeaway
The best-quantified component is consistency treatment (FEJ/OC/FEJ2): on EuRoC monocular it cuts average position ATE ~22% (0.181 -> 0.141 m, FEJ; 0.135 m FEJ2) and orientation ATE ~26%, and in noisy simulation the standard EKF's orientation NEES explodes (407 vs ~4). It is essentially free in compute (store first-estimate linearisation points, 0-10% overhead). Online calibration has no accuracy benefit when calibration is exact (our case) and matters only with bad priors. Gyro-compensated KLT with forward/backward checks, grid-balanced detection, chi² gating and careful IMU-noise scaling are cheap and important for fast motion; ZUPT by raw disparity is actively harmful on slow-moving drones per the course baseline.

### Cited Findings
- FEJ2 paper, EuRoC Table IV (10-run ATE deg/m, mono, 11 clones, ≤50 SLAM features): STD (no consistency fix) vs monoFEJ vs FEJ2 per sequence, e.g. V1_02 1.783/0.080 vs 0.574/0.052 vs 0.564/0.059; MH02 2.337/0.275 vs 0.920/0.156 vs 0.740/0.105; MH05 1.872/0.438 vs 1.212/0.347 vs 1.145/0.391. "OC, FEJ and FEJ2 produce smaller ATE than the standard EKF ... FEJ2 still achieves better accuracy on average, especially in monocular camera scenarios" — [FEJ2](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10376064).
- (Computed from that table, 11 sequences) mono average position ATE: STD 0.181 m, FEJ 0.141 m, FEJ2 0.135 m; mono average orientation ATE: STD 1.71°, FEJ 1.27°. Stereo differences are smaller (e.g. MH05 STD 0.352 vs FEJ 0.208).
- FEJ2 simulation (50 MC, mono/stereo, 225 m trajectory, 11 clones, 50/60 SLAM features): 1 px noise — STD ori RMSE 0.412°/pos 0.130 m with ori NEES 23.9; FEJ 0.242°/0.120 m, NEES 3.28; 3 px noise — STD 2.139°/0.402 m, ori NEES 407; FEJ 0.861°/0.289 m; FEJ2 0.650°/0.264 m; at 8 px "FEJ and OC diverge quickly due to the large linearization errors, while FEJ2 can still work" — [FEJ2](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10376064). Authors attribute monocular gains to "bad feature triangulation due to less visual constraints in the monocular case" causing larger linearisation errors.
- MSCKF 2.0: standard EKF Jacobians make the filter underestimate uncertainty; the fix (FEJ-style Jacobians preserving observability) achieves "accuracy and consistency higher than even an iterative, sliding-window fixed-lag smoother" in MC and real experiments — [LM13](https://intra.ece.ucr.edu/~mourikis/papers/Li2013IJRR.pdf) (abstract-level; table numbers not retrieved).
- Online calibration, [OV20] Table I (20 sim runs): true calib w/ online calib ATE 0.212°/0.134 m; true w/o 0.200°/0.128 m; bad w/ calib 0.218°/0.139 m; bad w/o calib 5.432°/508.719 m (diverged; pos NEES 1045) — [OV20](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/c10.pdf). The course baseline disables online calibration because intrinsics/extrinsics are exact — `OVSOL/solution.cpp`.
- KLT vs descriptors: OpenVINS supports both; KLT chosen for longer tracks (→ more SLAM features); FAST on a uniform grid; "one of the key weaknesses of indirect-based visual tracking methods in low gradient environments" — [OVFPV](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/r10.pdf). Outdoor needed uniform (grid) extraction more than indoor; stereo-constrained tracking failed due to too few features — [OVFPV](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/r10.pdf).
- Cloning/keyframing: "Keyframing is not used and instead we have a fixed sliding window size that always marginalize the oldest pose from our state vector and bounds the computational complexity" — [OVFPV](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/r10.pdf). (The course baseline's smoother instead keyframes at 5 Hz over 2.4 s — `OVSOL/fixed_lag_vio.h`.)
- Runtime split in OpenVINS FPV runs: "The time for each update is evenly split between visual feature tracking and update", 30-40 Hz on a laptop — [OVFPV](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/r10.pdf).
- Chi² gating: Li & Mourikis perform a Mahalanobis test on each nullspace-projected residual against the χ² percentile; joint use of all of a feature's observations in the MSCKF makes outliers easier to detect — [LM12](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss08/p31.pdf). Course baseline uses chi2 multiplier 1.0 for MSCKF/SLAM updates — `OVSOL/solution.cpp`.
- ZUPT: course baseline disables OpenVINS's disparity ZUPT after startup ("can mistake slow translation for rest and incorrectly absorb that motion into the IMU biases") — `OVSOL/README.md`.
- Dynamic/learned initialisation: [MERRILL23] (RSS 2023, OpenVINS group) replaces per-feature depths in the linear VI initialisation with scale+offset of a learned single-view depth map and reports improved initialisation on EuRoC/TUM-VI with windows as short as 300 ms on TUM-VI — [MERRILL23](https://roboticsconference.org/2023/program/papers/072/).
- S-MSCKF: stereo MSCKF for fast flight (up to 17.5 m/s), claims cost "comparable to state-of-art monocular solutions" and "significantly greater robustness" vs OKVIS/ROVIO/VINS-Mono on EuRoC — [SMSCKF](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00036); in [OV20] Table II stereo S-MSCKF averaged 0.130 m vs stereo OpenVINS 0.054 m.
- ROVIO (as described by [OV20]): monocular iterated EKF minimising direct photometric patch error, "allowing for tracking of non-corner features such as high gradient lines"; mono ROVIOLI average EuRoC ATE 0.140 m in [OV20] Table II — [OV20](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/c10.pdf).

### Inferences
- (Ranking, inference from the above) For a monocular, exactly-calibrated, fast-flight MSCKF: (1) relinearisation/consistency (FEJ or, better, iterated updates — the FEJ2 paper shows the remaining monocular error is linearisation from poor triangulation), (2) tracker robustness on fast turns (gyro-warped KLT, fwd/bwd check, grid balancing), (3) startup handling, (4) SLAM features, (5) online calibration (≈0 benefit with exact calibration).
- (Estimate) FEJ cost: storing first-estimate positions/velocities for each clone and using them in propagation Φ and measurement Jacobians — ~O(n) memory and no extra FLOPs beyond bookkeeping. FEJ2 adds a nullspace projection of a small Jacobian block per feature (≈ hundreds of ops per feature) — negligible vs our budget. An iterated EKF (2-3 relinearisations of the MSCKF update for the feature triangulation + Jacobian, without re-doing the covariance update) would cost ~2-3x the residual/Jacobian part only (Σℓ³-type term in LM12), probably +0.5-1 M ops/frame for 40-60 features — the cheap analogue of the baseline's 6 Ceres iterations.
- (Inference) Online extrinsic/time-offset calibration should not be ported: in the benchmark the calibration is exact and the timestamps synthetic; Table I shows no gain with true calibration and it adds 7-8+ states (raising n² costs).

### Gaps
- No published OpenVINS ablation isolating feature count, grid layout, KLT vs ORB accuracy, chi² threshold, clone count, or ZUPT on EuRoC/TUM-VI was found within budget. MINS and the OpenVINS initialisation journal (2024 SAGE/IJRR extension of [MERRILL23]) were not read.
- [LM13] numeric tables not retrieved (server refused connection).

## Q4. OpenVINS failure modes: fast motion, low texture, moving initialisation — why it can blow up on fast textureless indoor flights

### Takeaway
OpenVINS's own drone-racing report names the mechanism: FAST-on-grid + KLT finds few good corners on low-texture floors during high-speed straight segments, so the visual update starves and the filter drifts on the IMU; few features survive long enough to become SLAM anchors during dynamic motion; stock static initialisation needs a standstill/jerk and dynamic init needs parallax, so mid-flight starts are fragile. Single-linearisation EKF updates with poorly triangulated monocular features (FEJ2 paper) compound this.

### Cited Findings
- "Features are first extracted in a uniform grid using FAST detection, and as mentioned in the literature, is one of the key weaknesses of indirect-based visual tracking methods in low gradient environments ... We found that the Indoor 45° datasets had particular trouble during the high speed straight segments due to the lack of good feature extractions on the low texture floor regions. The use of direct-based visual feature tracking could possibly help address this." — [OVFPV](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/r10.pdf).
- "few SLAM features are tracked during highly dynamic motion" — [OVFPV](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/r10.pdf).
- Stock init: "The system self-initializes after detecting a change in the acceleration from being picked up at the beginning of each dataset" — [OVFPV](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/r10.pdf). The course port notes OpenVINS starts one thread per init attempt and patched it to synchronous; dynamic init can produce nonfinite input that made Eigen 3.4.0 JacobiSVD read out of bounds; extra guards added for nonfinite/nonpositive depths — `OVSOL/README.md`.
- A hovering camera can turn without baseline for triangulation; the course baseline added a rotation-only startup phase specifically for this — `OVSOL/README.md`, `rotation_update.h`.
- Motion blur: a 2025 comparison reports OpenVINS lost track on EuRoC V1_03 and V2_03 because of motion blur — [arXiv 2507.11241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11241) (search-snippet level; not read in full). [OV20] also excluded V2_03 "due the inability for some algorithms to run on it".
- User reports of divergence (Raspberry Pi 4/ROS2; also on EuRoC V1_01 per a commenter) — [issue #435](https://www.github.com/rpng/open_vins/issues/435); a fast downward-camera drone configuration question — [issue #418](https://www.github.com/rpng/open_vins/issues/418) (resolutions not retrieved).
- Monocular consistency: FEJ/OC "are more likely to suffer from bad feature triangulation due to less visual constraints in the monocular case. This leads to relatively larger linearization errors"; at 8 px noise FEJ/OC "diverge quickly" — [FEJ2](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10376064).
- Bad calibration without online calibration diverged to 508 m ATE in simulation — [OV20](https://udel.edu/~pgeneva/downloads/papers/c10.pdf) (not our failure mode: calibration is exact here).
- Server context: server flights travel ~60 m in 60 s (≈1 m/s mean) vs ~17 m in our pool — project memory server-flight-distribution.md; the course OpenVINS still reaches 0.553 m mean there — `MEM`.

### Inferences
- (Inference) Why OpenVINS-style filters do well on some flights and blow up on others: accuracy is gated by (#features with long, well-triangulated tracks)/time. Textured rooms + moderate speed → many long tracks → MSCKF + SLAM anchors constrain scale and velocity; low-texture + fast translation → corners vanish or blur, tracks shorten to 2-4 frames, triangulation baselines are tiny relative to depth uncertainty, chi² rejects or (worse) admits mis-linearised features, and velocity/scale random-walk on the IMU → the "slow drift/offset" pattern. Fast yaw turns additionally break un-warped KLT.
- (Inference) The course baseline's fixes map one-to-one onto these: gyro-warped KLT + fwd/bwd (fast turns), cohort-balanced landmark admission (coverage), keyframe-direct tracking (drift), relinearised window (bad triangulation), rotation-only startup (hover/turn starts), consensus (rare catastrophic branches).
- (Inference, for our filter) Highest value per op for our failure mode: (1) gyro-predicted KLT initial guess + fwd/bwd check if not already present; (2) low-texture fallback measurements that don't need corners (we already use lines/Manhattan; ROVIO-style direct patch residuals on gradients is the literature's suggested remedy, [OVFPV]/[OV20]); (3) keyframe-to-keyframe re-tracking at ~5 Hz; (4) iterated MSCKF update; (5) small capped SLAM-anchor set (10-20, 1-D inverse depth) for hover/turn segments; (6) cheap 2-3 back-end consensus sharing one frontend.
